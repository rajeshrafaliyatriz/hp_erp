## 1. What a top professional would do that you did not ask for

1. **Choose topics from learner data, then test each for simulation-fit.** Your tenant has 11 active people on 8 roles and 19 already-scored candidate topics. A pro does not pick "cell" or "Arduino" because they look good; they run a four-gate test: (a) the skill is a decision or causal-model skill, (b) practice with immediate feedback should beat reading, (c) an independent oracle can grade it, never the AI (a formula, a real tool, a rule book, or trained human raters), (d) demand: at least 2 people below the required level, or at least 4 people whose role needs it, or it is company-wide.
2. **Correct the premise first** (section 0), because the plan built on "PhET has 3D and AI" would be built on nothing.
3. **Build a runtime contract, not three demos.** A simulation is an immutable, versioned package (manifest + sandboxed module) plus a host-to-sim message contract, an event schema, a completion rule and per-tenant enablement. That same contract is what makes automated generation safe later: a generator emits a manifest and a validator can reject it.
4. **Treat "copy repos" as a legal and security gate before a technology choice.** Licence read at the exact commit, an attribution register, permissive-only shortlist, sandboxed iframe on a restricted origin, pinned versions, ask the author when there is no licence.
5. **Decide by measurement.** Build one vertical slice in the two or three best 3D engines plus a 2D control, and measure frame rate, bundle size, load time, authoring effort and AI first-time success on a throttled low-end profile against written budgets.
6. **Wrap learning around the simulation and measure it.** Predict, explore, explain, transfer; xAPI-shaped events from day one; a pilot with think-aloud, pre/post and a reading-only comparison, stating what 11 people can and cannot detect (about 0.8 to 0.9 standard deviations of the before/after change: your Step 1 design says 0.84, and an exact paired-t calculation for 11 people at 80% power gives 0.94 two-sided or 0.81 one-sided).
7. **Keep AI constrained.** AI proposes (hints, scenario drafts, manifest drafts) through one server-side gateway with schema-validated output and the existing balance guard; a deterministic engine grades; golden-set evals before any AI feature ships.
8. **Roll out safely and resumably.** Local first, live seeding last (reversible script + backup, no JSON columns), and a state file that survives a session reset.

## 4. What, other than you or me, shows the result is right

An "oracle" here means an independent source of the right answer: a formula, a real tool, a validator, an expert, a real user.

- **Maths and physics as oracle.** Worked example from your own screenshot: Helium (6.6e-27 kg) at 1000 m/s gives λ = h/(mv) ≈ 0.100 nm; reading the 0.06 µm label as the slit width, the first single-slit minimum sits at λL/a ≈ 1.0 mm at 0.60 m, so the central lobe is about 2 mm wide; at the screen's 5 mm bar (about 63 px) that is about 25 px, and the picture shows a band of about that width (my by-eye estimate, approximate). The 1.0 µm fringe spacing is 60 µm, under one pixel, which is why the screen looks like a smooth band. A simulation that cannot reproduce these numbers is wrong.
- **Real tools as oracle for IT topics.** The real `git` binary replayed in CI against the Git sandbox's graph; numpy/scikit-learn outputs against the ML sandbox; PGlite/Postgres result sets against SQL tasks; queueing formulas (Little's law, M/M/1) against the latency explorer.
- **Independent validators.** An xAPI statement validator for our events (and the ADL LRS conformance suite, MIT, if an xAPI server is added), Khronos glTF-Validator for any 3D files, axe-core, Lighthouse CI, licence scan, osv-scanner.
- **A named subject-matter expert per topic** signing a checklist (the same rule as your Step 1: pass needs a named expert's signature).
- **Real learners, with a comparison.** The 11 active people in tenant 6: think-aloud plus pre/post, and a reading-only condition (a crossover: half do the simulation first, half the reading or video first) with an unseen transfer task and kill criteria written in advance. Without a comparison, pre/post cannot show that a simulation beats reading. Be honest that 11 people only show large effects (about 0.8 to 0.9 standard deviations of the before/after change; an exact paired-t at 80% power gives 0.94 two-sided, 0.81 one-sided); a second stage with 100+ people is needed for small ones.
- **A blind visual comparison.** "Best visuals" is a matter of taste unless it is tested: 5 or more people outside the team rate the spike's screens against the reference videos without knowing which is which, with the pass threshold agreed before the test.
- **A real low-end device and a screen reader**, not the developer machine.

## Appendix C. Strawman of the runtime contract (engine-agnostic; Step 2 must confirm or replace it)

**Package manifest** (JSON in a LONGTEXT column, checked against a JSON Schema, immutable per version):
`id`, `version`, `schema`; `scope` (platform or tenant); `title` and `i18n` locales; `type` (parameter-explorer, scenario, sandbox, scene3d, roleplay); `engine` (adapter: js, pyodide, pglite or microvm; rendering library; minimum device tier); `entry` (URL + SHA-256, loaded in a sandboxed iframe on the package origin); `assets[]` (path, bytes, licence, source, attribution); `objectives[]` (competency ids); `flow` (predict, explore, explain, transfer); `parameters[]` (id, min, max, unit, default); `oracle` (kind: formula, reference-tool, rulebook or trained raters, plus a reference id) and a `solver` that CI replays to prove the check fails at the start and passes when solved; one checker contract for every adapter (state + event log in, pass/score/feedback out); `scoring` (pass mark, attempts, server_verified); `events[]` (the allowed event names); `hints` (ladder size; AI on/off and token budget); `a11y` (keyboard map, reduced-motion variant, text alternative); `estimated_minutes`.

**Messages** (postMessage, every one validated with zod; because a sandboxed frame's origin is "null", the host authenticates by `event.source` equal to its own iframe window plus a per-session nonce, never by origin alone, and never posts to `"*"`):
host to sim `init {lessonId, attemptId, nonce, locale, theme, reducedMotion, resume}`; sim to host `ready {manifestVersion}`, `event {seq, type, payload, ts}` (the host batches these, since the iframe has no token), `progress {step, percent}`, `hint.request {stateSnapshot}`, `finish {summary}`; host to sim `hint.reply {level, text}`. The server, not the sim, decides pass or fail by re-checking the events against the oracle.

**Tables** (raw DDL, LONGTEXT, no JSON, no foreign keys, migrations dated after 2026_12_02): `sim_package` (id, scope, tenant, version, manifest, sha256, status), `sim_attempt` (id, tenant, user, lesson, package version, started, finished, score, verified), `sim_event` (attempt, seq, type, payload). Each event also goes to `g2g_event` with idempotency key `sim:<attempt>:<seq>` (a repeat throws, so the writer catches it). Completion uses the existing progress call; a pass gate follows the `quizGate` pattern.

**Generation later:** a model drafts a manifest (and optionally a module) from a topic, competency and source facts; schema validation, oracle tests and static checks run automatically; an expert signs; the package is published as a new immutable version. Free-form generated code runs only in the sandboxed, separate-origin iframe.

(c) Tool-call edits to structured scene state: a 24-person study (Jul 2026) found users under-notice the AI's confabulations, so every AI edit must return the verified resulting state. **Decision: a validated, typed manifest is the default; reviewed code is the exception; check A2UI, json-render and MCP Apps for a format worth adopting before inventing one.** Gap: no benchmark exists for generated slider-style teaching simulations or IT-process simulations.
- *Validation gate order (proposed from the evidence).* Schema, then static checks (AST allow-list, units, ranges), then oracle / metamorphic / property tests, then a cross-solver differential test, then a headless render with an accessibility check, then expert sign-off, then an immutable versioned publish. "Runs without error" is a weak proxy; the effective checks read engine state or rebuild the model and compare it with the spec. Unsafe code is common: 45% of LLM outputs had OWASP Top-10 flaws (Veracode, Jul 2025) and 5.2% (commercial models) to 21.7% (open models) of suggested packages did not exist (USENIX Security 2025), so vendor every library and allow no runtime installs.
- *AI hints.* Scaffolded, content-grounded tutors help: Kestin et al. (Sci Rep, Jun 2025) 0.73 to 1.3 SD in intro physics with pre-written solutions given to the tutor; Bastani et al. Adversarial learners extract answers (small open tutors leaked 34 to 82%; reason-then-respond or a judge plus refiner cut it to 2 to 6%). Gap: no evidence yet for AI hints inside simulations specifically.
- *AI gateway.* Meter per tenant in our own database in addition to any gateway budget (Vercel AI Gateway offers budgets, fallbacks and logs). OpenAI announced it is acquiring promptfoo (MIT) on 9 Mar 2026 and says it will keep the open-source project; ClickHouse acquired Langfuse on 16 Jan 2026 and says it stays open source (both confirmed from the companies' own announcements): watch their direction, or use Inspect AI (MIT) or DeepEval (Apache-2.0). **E-6. Learning science, accessibility and performance (done).**
- *3D and game layers: the evidence tempers "best 3D".* Nothing shows that more realism or immersion adds learning. 3D helps spatial relations and procedural-motor content when the camera shows task-relevant views (3D beat other methods on spatial content, d 0.50; animation beat static for procedural-motor content, d 1.06; free rotation alone did not help). It is neutral or harmful for conceptual content, through extra load and seductive detail (a headset lab simulation gave more presence but less learning, d −0.80 on learning; a slideshow beat VR in another study). In games, schematic visuals matched or beat cartoon and realistic ones; gamification gains are small (g 0.49 cognitive, 0.25 behavioural), and a 355-employee study found intense gamification lowered autonomy and outcomes. **Rules: reserve 3D for spatial goals (system flow, structure, consequence); use schematic 2D for abstract IT content such as Git graphs, pipelines and timelines; stylised, not photoreal; curated camera presets plus a linked 2D view; self-explanation prompts; a game layer limited to feedback and progress, optional, with no narrative padding.** Gap: no study of desktop 3D against 2D on abstract IT topics.
- *Simulation effects and PhET's method.* Simulations beat no simulation (g 0.62 over 59 K-12 studies) and enhanced simulations beat plain ones (0.49); with scaffolds, simulation-based learning in higher education reached g 0.85; they work best as a supplement with support and reflection; guidance adds d 0.50. PhET's rules: it must work without instruction, learners manipulate the play area first, one explicit visual model, a minimal start-up state, little help, implicit scaffolding, and 4 to 6 think-aloud interviews per sim; learners will not explore without an incentive. Replicable here: all the rules, plus about 5 think-aloud sessions per simulation with target employees. No adult-workplace PhET studies exist.
- *Workplace learners.* Sitzmann (2011, 65 studies): self-efficacy +20%, declarative knowledge +11%, procedural +14%, retention +9%, larger when the simulation is active, replayable and a supplement (strong publication bias). Features that moved skills most (Cook 2013): range of difficulty and repetitive practice (0.68), distributed practice (0.66), interactivity (0.65), feedback (0.44); elaborated feedback beats right-or-wrong (0.49 against 0.05); fidelity adds only 1 to 2%, so match the task functionally, not physically. Measure beyond reaction: "useful for my job" predicts transfer better than enjoyment; transfer is stronger for open skills such as communication than closed ones such as software, and depends on a supportive workplace. Spaced beats massed practice (d 0.46). Gaps: no direct evidence on the best session length; no 2024 to 2026 serious-game meta-analysis for employees.
- *Accessibility target.* WCAG 2.2 AA (a W3C Recommendation; WCAG 3.0 is still a draft). EN 301 549 V3.2.1 is in force and V4.1.1 (published 2 Sep 2026) adopts WCAG 2.2 A/AA, with EU citation expected about 30 Nov 2026 (secondary sources). For 3D and canvas: keyboard operation and no trap (2.1.1, 2.1.2), name/role/value (4.1.2), text alternatives (1.1.1), focus visible and not obscured (2.4.7, 2.4.11), a dragging alternative and 24 px targets (2.5.7, 2.5.8), contrast 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for graphics (1.4.3, 1.4.11), pause for moving content over 5 seconds (2.2.2), no more than three flashes (2.3.1), status messages for AI chat (4.1.3), and `prefers-reduced-motion`. Reference implementations: PhET's parallel DOM inside its scene graph (interactive description, sonification, voicing) and Babylon's accessibility twin; a twin hidden with `display:none` is invisible to screen readers; test with NVDA.
- *Performance.* Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile: LCP up to 2.5 s, INP up to 200 ms, CLS up to 0.1. detect-gpu (MIT) gives tiers (0 = no WebGL or under 15 fps, then 15, 30 and 60 fps) but its benchmark data stopped updating in Dec 2025, so add a runtime frame-rate probe; Babylon's SceneOptimizer steps quality down to hold 60 fps. Khronos product-asset targets (2020) for a desktop web view are 3 MB, 250k triangles, under 100 draw calls and 2K textures (not whole-scene budgets). No data exists on the low-end share of corporate fleets (a proxy: about 40% of Windows PCs still ran Windows 10 in Sep 2025). Baseline: an integrated GPU, 60 fps with automatic drop to 30, a capped pixel ratio, lazy loading, a 2D or SVG fallback on tier 0, and telemetry of each tenant's real fleet.

**2. Validation pipelines.** Executing without error is a weak proxy; effective checks read engine state or rebuild the encoded model and compare it with the spec.
- PhysCodeEval: pass/fail from engine state with conservation-law residuals and expert assertions; supports cross-engine evaluation (a differential test) [P]. MooseBench rebuilds the PDE encoded in the code and scores it against the intended contract; a violation-report loop added +0.22 to +0.41 on hard cases [P]. LAMMPS: canonicalise, parse, run cheaply, then accuracy-check [P]. Metamorphic relations are proposed for the oracle problem [S] arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25043; no work was found pairing property-based testing with LLM-simulation invariants.

Package hallucination (USENIX Security 2025; 576k samples): 5.2% for commercial models against 21.7% for open models [P] usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/spracklen; a 2026 preprint reports 4.6 to 6.1% on newer models [U]. OWASP LLM05: treat model output as untrusted and apply strict CSP [P] genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm052025-improper-output-handling/.

**3. AI hints in simulations.** Scaffolded, content-grounded tutors help; unguarded chat can harm; answer leakage under adversarial learners is measured and reducible; engagement limits the effect.

**Q4. Documented architectures for a tenant-agnostic runtime.** The pattern is the same across projects: each unit is declared by a manifest and sandboxed (iframe or web component), talks to the host through a typed state and event contract, and LMS standards are handled outside the unit.
- Concord: Activity Player loads activity JSON from the LARA authoring API; each interactive is an iframe using `@concord-consortium/lara-interactive-api` v1.15.0 (MIT, built on `iframe-phone`) with typed InteractiveState, AuthoredState, DialogState and global state, plus `setHeight`, `setHint`, `getAuthInfo` and React hooks.
- PhET-iO: the host page embeds the sim in an iframe and calls `phetioClient.invoke(phetioID, method, args, cb)`; state is JSON keyed by phetioID; events form a hierarchical stream of user, model and wrapper events; versions are pinned `MAJOR.MINOR` and API changes are opt-in; Studio exports a customised launch page; the `phetsims/phet-io` repo returns 404 (not public).
- OLI Torus: each activity type has a `manifest.json` declaring `delivery`, `authoring` and `preview` custom elements with entry files, plus `schema.ts` and `transformations`. Oppia: `extensions/` holds plugin directories (40 interactions, rich-text components, visualisations, answer summarisers, value generators). Open edX XBlock: plugins load through Python entry points; field scopes are content, settings, user_state, preferences, user_info and user_state_summary; per-block iframes "grind to a halt" past about 10 blocks, so the Learning MFE iframes the whole unit for cookie isolation (2024-07-26).
- Standards: LTI 1.3 is OAuth2 plus JWT with Deep Linking 2.0, AGS and NRPS 2.0; cmi5 launch parameters are `endpoint`, `fetch`, `actor`, `registration` and `activityId`, its statements are launched, initialized, completed, passed, failed, terminated, abandoned, waived and satisfied, it has `moveOn` rules and is a profile of xAPI 1.0.3; xAPI 2.0 is IEEE 9274.1.1-2023 [S].
- Inferences: a workable contract is a manifest (id, version, entry, state schema), one sandboxed iframe per lesson step rather than per widget, and a typed message API (init, setState, emit, complete, hint); events map to cmi5/xAPI statements with platform-side adapters for LTI grade passback; scoring uses a server-side check function as in Instruqt and Killercoda. Gaps: no public doc of XBlock sandbox attributes; no permissive open-source AI-simulation generator found; Concord's multi-tenant model undocumented.

Gap: no 2025 to 2026 error-rate study for AI-generated 3D teaching assets.

**2. LLM code-generation fitness.** three.js and R3F have by far the deepest LLM footprint and official LLM docs, yet free-form 3D generation is still unreliable; PlayCanvas has the most first-party agent tooling.
- Footprint: npm weekly downloads are three 15.2M, R3F 4.25M and drei 2.76M against @babylonjs/core 235K and playcanvas 99.6K [2]; Stack Overflow tags three.js 20,863, react-three-fiber 1,021, babylonjs 465, playcanvas 19 (Babylon and PlayCanvas use their own forums, so SO undercounts them) [38].
- Official LLM docs: three.js has docs/llms.txt (5 KB) and llms-full (363 KB) [3]; R3F has llms-full (175 KB) [7]; Babylon has a 4 KB llms.txt only (llms-full returns 404) [1]; PlayCanvas has an "entry point for AI agents", a 3.18 MB llms-full, `npx skills add playcanvas/skills` and an Editor MCP server (MIT), and warns that Engine 1 code "often no longer works" [17].
- Benchmarks: WorldCoder-Bench (2026-06-01) has 2,026 tasks; across nine frontier models the best score is 27.8% on core and 19.9% on robust; failures are state drift and broken interaction chains; whether it is three.js-only is unconfirmed [32]. 3JSBench (2026-10-09, three.js) publishes no scores and its author runs a commercial platform (U) [33]. No Babylon, PlayCanvas, Unity or Godot LLM benchmark was found. Failure report: Babylon maintainers say assistants invent APIs and mix in other engines' APIs (2025-07, S) [14].
- Inference: automated generation should target a typed Sim Spec plus a vetted component kit rather than arbitrary engine code; R3F's declarative JSX suits that best; PlayCanvas's Editor MCP is the only first-party agent loop but ties you to the cloud Editor. Gap: no controlled cross-engine LLM comparison.

**3. Independent benchmarks (2025 to 2026).** None found that is independent; what exists is vendor-run or single-user.
- Galacean (vendor-run, M2 Pro, undated): 225 glTF models + 255 animations: Galacean 55 fps, Babylon.js 48 fps, three.js 4.5 fps; 500 particle systems: 57.6, 24.2 and 3 fps; Babylon's maintainer called it "unfair" because it set no performance-priority options [15][16] (S).
- three.js forum, r183 (2026-05-29, one user, RTX 4090 laptop, 4,000 meshes): WebGLRenderer 4.5 ms CPU and 32 ms first frame; WebGPURenderer 9.7 ms and 167 ms; no maintainer replied (S) [6]. Khronos glTF Render Fidelity covers three.js (via model-viewer), Babylon.js, filament.js, the glTF Sample Viewer, three-gpu-pathtracer and offline path tracers, with no scores, and PlayCanvas, Unity and Godot are absent [34]. A Utsubo comparison (S) has no measured fps and dates three.js WebGPU zero-config to "Sept 2025" although r171 shipped 2024-11-29 [1]: unreliable.
- Inference: visual ceilings are comparable (Babylon 9 has clustered and volumetric lights [10], PlayCanvas has CameraFrame [17], three.js has TSL post nodes [5] plus a path tracer (MIT) [1]); the real differences are effort and ecosystem. Gap: no low-end integrated-GPU or phone measurements exist for any engine.

**4. Education, accessibility, low-end.** PhET uses its own 2D scene graph, Scenery (MIT), with 3D added through three.js in `mobius` (pushed 2026-09-13) [35]. Labster uses Unity (vendor page; WebGL unconfirmed, S) [36]; EON Reality reportedly uses Babylon.js 8 (vendor PDF unparseable, U); no verified PlayCanvas, Godot or Wonderland education customers. WebGPU reach: Chrome and Edge 113+; Android Chrome 121+ (Android 12+, Qualcomm and ARM GPUs only); Safari and iOS 26; Firefox 141 on Windows and 145 on macOS ARM64 only; Linux in progress [30]. Adaptive quality: R3F has demand rendering and PerformanceMonitor [8], Babylon has SceneOptimizer [13]; Godot and Unity docs warn that mobile web is weaker than native [20][23]. Inference: canvas content is opaque to assistive technology in every engine, so keep essential content and controls in the DOM and treat 3D as a visual layer; Babylon offers the most built-in help.

**5. Ranked recommendation.** (1) three.js + R3F + drei (MIT): the React-native model, React 19 peers, the smallest base weight, the deepest LLM and ecosystem footprint and official LLM docs; risks: you assemble the "engine" yourself, WebGPU APIs churn and official R3F WebGPU is still v10 alpha, the 2026 release cadence is slower, accessibility is DIY. (2) Babylon.js 9 (Apache-2.0): batteries-included (Havok, GUI, editors, SceneOptimizer, the accessibility twin, WebGPU parity); risks: a large bundle, a small React wrapper, a weaker LLM footprint. (3) PlayCanvas: choose it if non-engineers must author scenes collaboratively and the roughly $50 per seat a month cloud lock-in is acceptable; its React wrapper is pre-1.0. For game-like feel with the best visuals and a small React/TypeScript team: R3F. For AI-automated generation: R3F with a constrained Sim Spec; PlayCanvas is the runner-up for vendor agent tooling. Architecture note: several of the eight topics (Git, testing, incidents, sales pipeline) are 2D diagrams, and PhET is mostly 2D; make 3D one renderer behind the Sim Spec and consider PixiJS 8 for 2D game-like sims. Gaps: real tree-shaken bundle sizes need a build (not run); prices for Unity Industry and Needle Pro; the Havok binary licence text; Epic's primary terms (403); Next 16-specific R3F reports.

### D-LEARNING. When 3D, immersion and gamification help or hurt, and the design and accessibility rules that follow (researcher 6; as of 26 Oct 2026)

Tags: [P] primary text read (paper abstract or text, standard, vendor docs); [S] secondary summary; [U] unverified or inference. Most learning-science evidence dates from 2005 to 2020; 2025 to 2026 items are labelled. DOI check: every DOI cited resolves in Crossref. Four DOIs first recalled were wrong (Keehner 2008, Krüger 2022, Plass 2014, Lancaster 2013); Keehner and Krüger were corrected through Crossref title search and Plass and Lancaster are not cited. Adams et al. 2008 (JILR) and the D'Angelo 2014 SRI report have no DOI; the arXiv paper resolves via DataCite only.

**1. When 3D, immersion and game layers help or hurt.** No evidence was found that more realism or immersion adds learning. 3D helps for spatial relations and procedural-motor content when the camera shows task-relevant views; it is neutral or harmful for conceptual content, through extra load and seductive detail; schematic visuals match or beat realistic ones in game meta-analyses.
- Makransky 2019 (doi:10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.12.007), headset lab simulation against desktop, N=52: presence d=1.30 higher, learning d=0.80 lower, EEG load d=0.59 higher [S]. Parong and Mayer 2018 (doi:10.1037/edu0000241): a slideshow beat VR on the posttest and VR was rated more engaging; segmenting plus summaries helped VR; no effect size retrieved [P]. Makransky and Mayer 2019 to 2021 (doi:10.1037/edu0000473), N=131 and 165: immersive VR against video gave more presence and enjoyment and no learning gain; enactment raised procedural (ηp² .144) and transfer (.088) in immersive VR only [P].
- Headset meta-analyses [P/S]: Wu 2020 (doi:10.1111/bjet.13023, 35 studies) ES 0.24 against non-immersive; Coban 2022 (doi:10.1016/j.edurev.2022.100452, 48 studies) g 0.38 overall and 0.30 against computer-assisted learning; a 2025 teacher-education meta-analysis (doi:10.3389/frvir.2025.1620905, 22 studies) g 0.52 with headsets 0.65 against desktop 0.18 (I²=72%, classroom and social skills). A 2026 field study (Computers in Human Behavior, doi:10.1016/j.chb.2025.108853) found immersive VR gave higher load and lower learning (abstract snippet only) [S].
- 3D against other methods [P]: Yammine 2015 (doi:10.1002/ase.1510, 36 studies) spatial d 0.50, factual 0.30; Krüger 2022 (doi:10.1016/j.caeo.2021.100065, N=150) AR 3D beat 2D for spatial relations with higher germane load and high mental-rotation learners gained more; Höffler and Leutner 2007 (doi:10.1016/j.learninstruc.2007.09.013) animation against static d 0.37, realistic 0.76, procedural-motor 1.06. Keehner 2008 (doi:10.1080/03640210801898177): free rotation did not help by itself; seeing task-relevant views did, whether active or passive.
- Seductive details [P]: Harp and Mayer 1998 (doi:10.1037/0022-0663.90.3.414); Rey 2012 (doi:10.1016/j.edurev.2012.05.003, 39 effects): small-to-medium harm to retention and medium to transfer; Wong and Adesope 2021 (doi:10.1007/s10648-020-09545-x): integral emotional design helped (retention g+ .35, transfer .27).
- Games [P]: Clark 2016 (doi:10.3102/0034654315582065, via the SRI summary): g 0.33 (k=57), schematic beat cartoon and realistic games, realism plus narrative had a small negative relation to learning, multi-session gains tied to spacing; Wouters 2013 (doi:10.1037/a0031311): learning d .29, retention .36, motivation .26 (n.s.), better when supplemented, multi-session or group play.
- Simulations [P]: D'Angelo 2014 (SRI brief, 59 K-12 studies 1991 to 2012): simulation against none g+ .62, enhanced against plain simulation .49; Chernikova 2020 (doi:10.3102/0034654320933544, 145 studies): g .85, scaffolds add, low prior knowledge benefits from examples and high from reflection; Smetana and Bell 2012 (doi:10.1080/09500693.2011.605182, 61 studies): best as a supplement with support and reflection; Rutten 2012 (doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2011.07.017) and de Jong 2013 (doi:10.1126/science.1230579): reviews with no pooled ES; simulations enhance instruction, especially labs, and virtual and physical should be combined.
- Gamification [P]: Sailer and Homner 2020 (doi:10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w) g .49 cognitive, .36 motivational, .25 behavioural (only cognitive stable); a 2026 higher-education meta-analysis (doi:10.1177/21582440261421375) d 1.12 but I²=98.8%, so low weight; Eger 2025 (doi:10.1080/1359432x.2024.2412360), N=355 employees: higher gamification intensity lowered autonomy and outcomes.
- Inference [U]: reserve 3D for spatial goals; use schematic 2D for abstract IT content such as pipelines, Git graphs and incident timelines; provide curated camera presets and linked 2D views; add self-explanation prompts; keep the game layer to feedback and progress, optional, with no narrative padding. Gaps: no study of desktop 3D against 2D on abstract IT topics and no 2025 desktop-3D meta-analysis; Höffler 2010 and Merchant 2014 abstracts not retrieved; Mayer's coherence effect-size table was paywalled.

**2. PhET.** Its gains come from intuitive direct manipulation, minimal text and help, implicit scaffolding and repeated interviews with target users. The evidence is physics and chemistry at K-12 and undergraduate level (2005 to 2014); no adult-workplace PhET studies were found.
- Process [P] (design process paper, 2009; research page): a team of 3 to 5 (developer, scientist, teacher, education researcher); learning goals first; a sim stays "under construction" until interviews show engagement and learning, then goes to classroom trial; 2 to 4 formal studies a year; each sim gets 4 to 6 think-aloud interviews; the 2008 corpus held over 200 interviews and over 60 sims (Adams 2008 Part II).
- Principles [P]: the sim must work without instruction or attention goes to the interface; students manipulate play-area objects first (about 10 minutes before touching the control panel); use an explicit visual model with cartoon-like real-world cues that "break" sensibly; novices weigh all visual cues equally, so remove irrelevant ones; start-up state should be minimal (an impressive Radio Waves start overwhelmed students); avoid help, and if needed keep it brief, on request and beside the item. Implicit scaffolding [P] (Podolefsky 2013, arXiv 1306.6544): affordances, constraints, cueing and feedback "guide without students feeling guided".
- Outcomes [P]: Finkelstein 2005 (doi:10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.1.010103): simulated circuits beat real equipment on concepts and on assembling a real circuit; Chamberlain 2014 (doi:10.1039/C4RP00009A), n=210: exploratory-feature use was 85% under light, 68% under moderate and 9% under heavy guidance; Moore 2013 (doi:10.1039/C3RP20157K), n=80, no instructions: students used 18 of 23 features; Lazonder and Harmsen 2016 (doi:10.3102/0034654315627366), 72 studies: guidance d=.50; PhET notes students will not explore without an incentive.
- Replicable [U]: all the design rules, plus about 5 think-aloud sessions per sim with target employees; the classroom validation history cannot be replicated. Licence: PhET HTML sims published before 29 Mar 2026 are CC BY 4.0, which permits commercial use with attribution and logo; terms for newer sims could not be retrieved [U].

**3. Workplace learners.** Simulations work best as active, repeatable, feedback-rich supplements; entertainment value adds little; effects shrink at higher Kirkpatrick levels, so measure beyond reaction.
- Sitzmann 2011 (doi:10.1111/j.1744-6570.2011.01190.x) [P]: 65 studies, N=6,476; self-efficacy 20% higher, declarative 11%, procedural 14%, retention 9%; strong publication bias; gains larger when content was delivered actively, replays were unlimited and the simulation supplemented other instruction; simulations lost to an active comparison.
- Kirkpatrick-level evidence [P]: Arthur 2003 (doi:10.1037/0021-9010.88.2.234) d≈.60 for reaction (k=15), learning (k=234), behaviour (k=122) and results (k=26); Alliger 1997 (doi:10.1111/j.1744-6570.1997.tb00911.x): "useful for my job" reactions predict transfer better than enjoyment; Cook 2011 (doi:10.1001/jama.2011.1234; health professions, 609 studies): knowledge 1.20, behaviours about 0.8, patient outcomes 0.50.
- Design features for skills outcomes [P] (Cook 2013, doi:10.3109/0142159X.2012.714886): range of difficulty .68, repetitive practice .68, distributed practice .66, interactivity .65, multiple strategies .62, feedback .44. Feedback [P]: Gegenfurtner 2014 (doi:10.1111/bjet.12188, k=15): more user control helped, assessment feedback after training beat feedback during it, social, narrative and multimedia features were not significant; van der Kleij 2015 (doi:10.3102/0034654314564881): elaborated feedback .49, correct answer .32, right/wrong .05, delay hurt on average.
- Realism [P]: Norman 2012 (doi:10.1111/j.1365-2923.2012.04243.x, 24 studies): high-fidelity advantage only 1 to 2%; Hamstra 2014 (doi:10.1097/ACM.0000000000000130) argues for functional task alignment over physical resemblance. Transfer [P]: Blume 2010 (doi:10.1177/0149206309352880, 89 studies): predictors are ability, conscientiousness, motivation and a supportive work environment; effects stronger for open skills such as communication than closed skills such as software. Session length [P]: Donovan 1999 (doi:10.1037/0021-9010.84.5.795) spaced beat massed (d=.46); a 2025 microlearning review (doi:10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e41413, 40 studies, segments of about 1 to 15 minutes) was positive but gave no pooled ES. Gaps: no direct evidence on optimal session length; no 2024 to 2026 serious-game meta-analysis for employees; Kirkpatrick Partners' site was blocked.

**4. Accessibility.**
- WCAG 2.2 is the W3C Recommendation (this text dated 12 Dec 2024; first published 5 Oct 2023); WCAG 3.0 is still a Working Draft (10 Sep 2026), so do not target it [P]. EN 301 549 V3.2.1 (2021) is in force and based on WCAG 2.1; V4.1.1 was published 2 Sep 2026 and adopts WCAG 2.2 A/AA (Deque, 8 Sep 2026; DWT, 18 Sep 2026) [S]; a presumption of conformity needs citation in the EU Official Journal, which Deque forecasts for about 30 Nov 2026 (an earlier snippet said 16 Dec 2026, unverified); the ETSI PDF was not reachable.
- Success-criteria checklist for 3D and canvas [P]: 1.1.1 text alternatives, 4.1.2 name/role/value, 2.1.1 keyboard, 2.1.2 no trap (all A); 2.4.7 focus visible and 2.4.11 focus not obscured (AA); 2.5.1 pointer gestures (A); 2.5.7 dragging alternative and 2.5.8 24×24 px targets (AA); 1.4.1 colour, 1.4.3 contrast 4.5:1, 1.4.11 contrast 3:1 for graphics, 1.4.4 and 1.4.10 resize and reflow; 2.2.1 timing, 2.2.2 pause moving content over 5 seconds, 2.3.1 three flashes; 2.3.3 disabling interaction-triggered motion (Level AAA, so optional under EN 301 549); 1.2.2 captions, 1.2.5 audio description, 4.1.3 status messages (for AI chat and feedback); `prefers-reduced-motion` has been Baseline widely available since Jan 2020 [P].
- W3C XR requirements [P]: the XAUR note (25 Aug 2021, not baseline requirements) asks for object query, mutable animations, large targets, adjustable speed, no seizure triggers and text for audio. PhET reference implementation [P]: a parallel DOM inside a custom scene graph gives keyboard and screen-reader access (W4A 2018, doi:10.1145/3192714.3192817) and uses an auditory-description framework (CHI 2020, doi:10.1145/3313831.3376460); its features are alternative input, interactive description, sonification and voicing (the feature list is [S] because the PhET accessibility page did not render). Babylon.js [P]: the accessibility package builds an HTML twin of tagged objects, supports only three pick triggers and says to tag only what matters; a forum report of lag with thousands of meshes is [S]. Typical WebGL failures [S] (three.js forum practitioners, Sep to Oct 2025): the canvas carries no semantics and a twin hidden with `display:none` is invisible to screen readers; fixes they suggest are a DOM mirror, a "warp" keyboard mode, live announcements and testing with NVDA; mapping these failures to specific success criteria is the researcher's inference [U].

**5. Performance and devices.** Global support [P]: WebGL 97.5%, WebGL2 97.1%, WebGPU 85.8% plus 3.7% partial (caniuse, fetched 26 Oct 2026); WebGPU ships in Chrome and Edge 113+, Firefox 141 (Windows) and 145 (macOS ARM) and Safari 26; Linux, Intel Mac and Android support is partial (web.dev, 25 Nov 2025). Budgets [P]: Core Web Vitals (page updated 31 Oct 2024) LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms, CLS ≤0.1 at the 75th percentile. Adaptive tiers [P]: detect-gpu (MIT): tier 0 is no WebGL, blocklisted or under 15 fps, with a non-WebGL fallback; tiers 1 to 3 are ≥15, ≥30 and ≥60 fps; its GFXBench data stopped updating in Dec 2025. Babylon's SceneOptimizer targets 60 fps and re-checks every 2 s, degrading in this order: merge meshes and shadows, then post-processes and particles, then textures, then render targets, then hardware scaling. Asset budgets [P]: Khronos v1.0 (Oct 2020) sets, for a desktop 3D web view, 3 MB, 250k triangles, under 100 draw calls and 2K textures, and for a per-item planning tool 1 MB, 40k triangles and under 5 draw calls (product-asset targets, not whole-scene budgets, U); Khronos 2.0 (7 Aug 2025) gives no numbers and recommends Draco and MeshOpt compression. Fleet data [S]: no 2025 to 2026 data on the low-end share of corporate fleets; the only proxy is that about 40% of Windows PCs still ran Windows 10 in Sep 2025 (StatCounter via Gigazine, 15 Oct 2025); no memory-budget source found. Inference [U]: take an integrated GPU as the baseline, target 60 fps with automatic drop to 30, cap device pixel ratio, lazy-load assets and fall back to 2D or SVG on tier 0; measure each tenant's real fleet with telemetry.
