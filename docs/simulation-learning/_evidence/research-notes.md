### D-AI. AI-generated simulations and AI help (researcher 5)

**1. Generating simulations from prompts.** Three architectures are in use: free-form code (flexible, measured accuracy poor), a declarative manifest over a trusted component catalog (safe by construction), and tool-call edits to structured scene state. Code that runs is often physically wrong.
- Gemini builds interactive simulations and 3D models in chat; Workspace rollout 24 Aug 2026, on by default, no accuracy caveats stated. [P] workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/08/generate-interactive-simulations-and-models-in-the-Gemini-app.html
- Benchmarks (hard solver or 3D tasks): SimuScene (11 Feb 2026, v2 Aug): 334 human-verified tests, 10 LLMs, best 21.5% [P] arxiv.org/abs/2602.10840. PhysCodeBench (v2 11 Sep 2026): best proprietary baseline 23.8% physical-assertion pass, a targeted multi-agent corrector 70.6% [P] arxiv.org/abs/2604.23580. WorldSolver (6 Oct 2026): 168 tasks, top agents 48.7% and 46.7% [P] arxiv.org/abs/2610.08720. MooseBench (10 May 2026): after execution-only repair, 39 to 40% of 220 cases still ran but solved the wrong physics [P] arxiv.org/abs/2605.09360. LAMMPS (21 Mar 2026): parser pass 74% to 91%, but only 1 of 80 scripts on the hardest prompt fully correct [P] arxiv.org/abs/2603.20630.
- Educational HTML/JS simulations (Dec 2024, older models o1 and Claude 3.5 Sonnet): a pendulum's analytic curve showed half the true period; identical prompts gave different outputs; "some corrections through human supervision typically remain necessary" [S] arxiv.org/html/2412.07482v1.
- Manifest route: A2UI (15 Dec 2025, Apache-2.0, v0.8, "early"; declarative data, the client holds the trusted catalog) [P] developers.googleblog.com/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/; json-render (Vercel Labs, Apache-2.0) [P] github.com/vercel-labs/json-render; MCP Apps (spec 2026-01-26; HTML UIs in sandboxed iframes, JSON-RPC over postMessage) [P] github.com/modelcontextprotocol/ext-apps.
- Tool-call scene editing: a 24-user study of LLM-assisted 3D editing (25 Jul 2026) taxonomises confabulations and finds a "perception-reality gap" [P] arxiv.org/abs/2607.23213.
- Inferences: a validated typed manifest should be the default and reviewed code the exception; every AI edit tool should return the verified resulting state. Gaps: no benchmark for LLM-generated slider-type educational simulations or IT-process simulations; no error rates published by Google or PhET; PhysCodeBench v1 reportedly had 700 samples against 1,200 in v2 [S].

**2. Validation pipelines.** Executing without error is a weak proxy; effective checks read engine state or rebuild the encoded model and compare it with the spec.
- PhysCodeEval: pass/fail from engine state with conservation-law residuals and expert assertions; supports cross-engine evaluation (a differential test) [P]. MooseBench rebuilds the PDE encoded in the code and scores it against the intended contract; a violation-report loop added +0.22 to +0.41 on hard cases [P]. LAMMPS: canonicalise, parse, run cheaply, then accuracy-check [P]. Metamorphic relations are proposed for the oracle problem [S] arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25043; no work was found pairing property-based testing with LLM-simulation invariants.
- Unsafe code: Veracode (30 Jul 2025; 80 tasks, 100+ LLMs): 45% of outputs had OWASP Top-10 flaws, XSS unsecured in 86%, larger models no better [P]. Package hallucination (USENIX Security 2025; 576k samples): 5.2% for commercial models against 21.7% for open models [P] usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/spracklen; a 2026 preprint reports 4.6 to 6.1% on newer models [U]. OWASP LLM05: treat model output as untrusted and apply strict CSP [P] genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm052025-improper-output-handling/.
- Review burden: tutors approved 76.4% of AI drafts with minimal or no edits (29 Dec 2025) [P] arxiv.org/abs/2512.23633. Isolated test runs: Vercel Sandbox uses Firecracker microVMs for untrusted code (docs updated 22 Sep 2026) [P] vercel.com/docs/vercel-sandbox.
- Proposed gate order: schema, static checks (AST allow-list, units and ranges), oracle / metamorphic / property tests, cross-solver diff, headless render with accessibility check, expert sign-off, immutable versioned publish. IT-topic simulations can use real git, pytest or SQL as oracles. Gap: no published false-negative rates for any pipeline.

**3. AI hints in simulations.** Scaffolded, content-grounded tutors help; unguarded chat can harm; answer leakage under adversarial learners is measured and reducible; engagement limits the effect.
- Kestin et al. RCT (Sci Rep, 3 Jun 2025; 194 analysed, crossover): median post-test 4.5 against 3.5, effect 0.73 to 1.3 SD; the tutor was given pre-written step-by-step solutions to curb hallucination; hallucination rate not measured; intro physics only [P] pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/.
- Bastani et al. (PNAS, Jul 2025; about 1,000 students): practice scores rose 48% (GPT Base) and 127% (GPT Tutor); after access was removed GPT Base fell 17%, and the guardrails largely mitigated this [S] (PNAS returned 403; figures from an abstract mirror).
- Pardos and Bhandari (PLOS ONE, May 2024, older): ChatGPT hints failed quality checks on 32% of problems; self-consistency cut this to about 0% (algebra) and 13% (statistics) [P].
- Khanmigo (two-year cluster RCT, 18 schools, Aug 2026): about 0.06 to 0.08 SD per year; "the binding constraint appears to be engagement" [P] edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1551. Eedi/LearnLM (n=165): 66.2% against 60.7% on novel problems, with human supervision [P].
- Answer leakage (20 Apr 2026, adversarial student agents): small open tutors leaked 34 to 82%, GPT-5 4.58%; reason-then-respond, or a judge plus a refiner, cut leakage to 2 to 6% for some models; persuasion beat adversarial tricks; the LLM judge scored kappa 0.81 to 0.88 against humans [P]. MathTutorBench: solving ability does not equal teaching ability [S]. CodeHelp (2023): sufficiency check, response, then a code-removal pass [S]. LLM-judge surveys list position and other biases [S].
- Inferences: a hint ladder (nudge, concept, targeted, worked step) gated by simulation state; hints generated from the author's reference solution plus a state snapshot; a second-pass leakage check; a golden set with adversarial learner turns; a human audit sample; rubric judges calibrated per rubric. Gap: no evidence for AI hints inside simulations or games specifically.

**4. Multi-tenant AI gateway.** One server-side policy layer with the tenant id on every call, hard caps, PII scrubbing and logging; pin everything; several popular components are open-core or changed owner in 2026.
- Vercel AI Gateway (docs 14 Sep 2026): budgets for team, project, key or member with soft caps; provider and model fallbacks; request logs; zero markup; BYOK spend is not counted toward budgets [P] vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway.
- LiteLLM: key, team and user budgets and rate limits (per-model budgets are Enterprise-only) [P]; compromised releases v1.82.7 and v1.82.8 (24 Mar 2026) [P] securitylabs.datadoghq.com and v1.107.0 (21 Oct 2026; root cause preliminary) [P] docs.litellm.ai/blog/security-incident-oct-21-2026.
- OpenAI API: abuse logs kept up to 30 days and no training by default; zero data retention needs approval; EU regional storage available; Anthropic and Google terms not checked [P]. Vercel AI SDK: Zod validation of tool inputs and `stopWhen` step caps [P] ai-sdk.dev/docs/foundations/tools.
- Prompt injection: OpenAI (Dec 2025) says it is "unlikely to ever be fully solved" [S]; a six-pattern paper (10 Jun 2025) recommends application-specific agents and combining patterns [P] arxiv.org/abs/2506.08837; OWASP 2025 lists LLM01 injection, LLM05 output handling, LLM06 excessive agency, LLM09 misinformation, LLM10 unbounded consumption [P]. EU AI Act: the AI Omnibus moves Annex III (education and employment) high-risk duties to 2 Dec 2027 (Council 29 Jun 2026) [S].
- Licences (gh api and LICENSE files, 26 Oct 2026): Apache-2.0: AI SDK, Vercel Sandbox SDK, A2UI, json-render, MCP Apps, NeMo Guardrails, Guardrails AI, garak, DeepEval, E2B, Firecracker, gVisor, Playwright, DOMPurify. MIT: Presidio, Inspect AI, fast-check, Zod, Ajv, CopilotKit, AG-UI. LiteLLM MIT but `enterprise/` is commercial (open-core; two 2026 compromises). Langfuse MIT but `ee/` is commercial (ClickHouse acquisition announced Jan 2026). promptfoo MIT (OpenAI acquisition announced 9 Mar 2026). MPL-2.0: Pyodide, axe-core, Hypothesis. Semgrep engine LGPL-2.1; Semgrep rules under a Rules License (internal business purposes, not "as a service"): avoid bundling in a SaaS. LLM Guard MIT but archived Jul 2026: avoid. Sandpack Apache-2.0 but last push Apr 2025. Not reviewed: Llama Guard, WebContainers.
- Inferences: meter per tenant in our own database in addition to gateway budgets; cache only tenant-scoped static hints; log hashed ids, not raw PII; a per-tenant kill switch; pin dependencies by digest. Gaps: no evidence on semantic-cache leakage; the AI Omnibus Official Journal date is unverified.

**5. Safe execution of generated front-end code.** Treat generated code as hostile: a separate origin inside a sandboxed iframe, with CSP and a strict postMessage protocol, or avoid code entirely with manifests.
- MDN iframe: `allow-scripts` plus `allow-same-origin` on a same-origin embed "lets the embedded document remove the sandbox attribute"; MDN recommends a separate origin and offers `credentialless` and `csp` attributes [P]. MDN postMessage: verify `origin` and `source`, validate message syntax, never use "*" as the target [P]. Null-origin pitfall: sandboxed frames report origin "null", so naive equality checks pass [S].
- MCP Apps spec: the host and the sandbox proxy MUST have different origins; default CSP `default-src 'none'; connect-src 'none'`; the host SHOULD log CSP configurations [P].
- Real attacks: Open WebUI CVE-2026-87995 (CVSS 8.7, 9 Sep 2026), account takeover via an iframe with both flags [P] (I confirmed the record in NVD); three other 2026 CVEs share the root cause [S]. Anthropic artifacts (Aug 2024, older): sandboxed iframes with site isolation and strict CSP [S]. Willison test (3 Apr 2026): a meta-tag CSP inside a `sandbox="allow-scripts"` iframe could not be removed in Chromium or Firefox; versions not given, Safari untested [S].
- Inferences: a dedicated cookie-less sandbox domain on object storage with CSP headers; `sandbox="allow-scripts"` only; an allow-list of message types with a schema; vendor the libraries (no CDN, no runtime package installs). Gaps: CPU, memory and infinite-loop limits not researched; Safari behaviour unknown.

### D-LABS. Hands-on IT labs and in-browser sandboxes (researcher 4)

Legend: P primary page or repo read; S secondary; U unverified or inference; licences and push dates from `gh api` on 2026-10-26; "calc" is the researcher's arithmetic on the cited rates.

**A. In-browser runtimes.** Pyodide, JupyterLite, PGlite, SQLite/DuckDB-WASM and hand-built JS simulators are commercially clean and maintained. WebContainers and CheerpX need paid licences even for internal or employee use. Sandpack's Node runtime is restricted and stale.
- Pyodide 314.0.7, Python 3.14.2, released 2026-09-14 (MPL-2.0, file-level copyleft): no threads or multiprocessing; sockets non-functional, network only via browser fetch/CORS [P] pyodide.org/en/stable/usage/wasm-constraints.html; build max memory 4 GB, about 2 GB reported in practice [S]; bundles scikit-learn 1.8.0 (upstream is 1.9.1), numpy, scipy, pandas, pytest, fastapi, duckdb; no torch, hypothesis or mutmut [P] cdn.jsdelivr.net/pyodide/v314.0.7/full/pyodide-lock.json; cold download measured at about 31 MB (runtime 9.6, scipy 14.0, scikit-learn 4.4, numpy 3.0) plus 4.2 for pandas; stopping runaway code needs a worker plus SharedArrayBuffer headers [P]; runs under Node [P].
- JupyterLite (BSD-3): static hosting, Web Worker kernels, "not all features" of JupyterLab [P]; the scikit-learn docs build supports JupyterLite links [P]. PGlite (Apache-2.0/PostgreSQL): about 3 MB gzipped, single user and connection, IndexedDB persistence [P]. Both pushed Oct 2026.
- SQLite-WASM and DuckDB-WASM (MIT); xterm.js (MIT; only the terminal UI); just-bash (Apache-2.0, beta simulated bash over an in-memory filesystem). SQLite's OPFS VFS needs COOP/COEP headers; `opfs-sahpool` does not but is single-connection [P].
- v86 (BSD-2) has no 64-bit support; container2wasm (Apache-2.0) is "experimental", emulated, with CORS-bound networking and GPL/LGPL parts in its output: toy use only (U).
- WebContainers (proprietary): "Licensing is required for production usage of the API in a commercial, for-profit setting", extended to meeting the needs of "employees"; no price [P] webcontainers.io/enterprise; Chrome full, Firefox and Safari beta, needs cross-origin isolation [P, Oct 2026]. CheerpX (proprietary engine; WebVM repo Apache-2.0): free only for individuals, FOSS and evaluation; a company's internal app and any self-hosting need a commercial licence; no price [P, 2026] cheerpx.io/docs/licensing.
- Sandpack (Apache-2.0) last push 2025-04-24; Nodebox last push 2023-11-29 under a Sustainable Use License ("own internal business purposes or non-commercial" only) [P].
- Inferences: browser runtimes cost about $0 marginal; browser-side grading can be tampered with, so re-run hidden checks server-side for anything certification-grade. Gaps: the real Pyodide memory ceiling; FastAPI inside Pyodide; WebContainers and CheerpX prices.

**B. Server-side sandboxes.** MicroVM sandboxes cost roughly $0.05 to $0.17 per learner-hour; the real costs are authoring, abuse control and operations.
- Vercel Sandbox (SDK Apache-2.0): Firecracker microVM with its own kernel; active CPU $0.128/h plus $0.0212/GB-h, so 1 vCPU and 2 GB is $0.055/h at 10% CPU and $0.170/h at 100% (calc; docs 2026-09-10); Pro allows 24 h sessions, Hobby 45 min and 10 concurrent; outbound open by default and restrictable; exposed ports are public (docs 2026-08-25). For 11 learners at 10 h each: $6 to $19 a month (calc).
- E2B: 2 vCPU and 4 GiB about $0.166/h, Pro $150/month. AWS Fargate x86: 1 vCPU and 2 GB about $0.049/h (calc). Firecracker (Apache-2.0): under 5 MiB overhead, under 125 ms boot, used by Lambda, E2B and Fly.io. gVisor (Apache-2.0): user-space kernel, poor for syscall-heavy work, incomplete syscall coverage. Kata (Apache-2.0).
- Killercoda: Ubuntu 24.04 with Docker/Podman, kubeadm clusters, 1 h free and 4 h paid sessions, public unguessable URLs; prices and embedding undocumented [P]. Instruqt: quote-based [S]; LMS embed via callbacks, webhooks and one-time play tokens; no SCORM, xAPI or LTI mentioned [P] docs.instruqt.com/settings/integrations. KodeKloud's checker is undocumented [S].
- Self-hosted: Play with Docker (MIT): hosted service "unavailable starting March 1, 2026" [P]. HobbyFarm (Apache-2.0) last push 2025-11-26. CTFd (Apache-2.0) checks static or regex flags only [P].
- Vulnerable targets: Juice Shop (MIT) one instance per user, "might crash under the load" [P]. MultiJuicer (Apache-2.0): 0.2 CPU and 300 MB per team plus 1 CPU and 1 GB overhead [P], so under $0.01/h per learner (calc). WebGoat is GPL-2.0-or-later [P].
- Inferences: abuse controls (egress deny-list, TTL, CPU and memory quotas, one VM per session, no shared credentials); free hosted labs are a vendor risk. Gaps: Instruqt, Killercoda and KodeKloud prices; iximiuz Labs' check mechanism.

**C. Recommendation per topic.**
- Git: in-browser model for concepts (Learn Git Branching is 100% client-side with its own `GitEngine` and "git golf" [P]); add isomorphic-git for real merge and conflict drills (it has merge, cherryPick and stash but no rebase [P]); avoid wasm-git (libgit2, GPLv2). About $0; fidelity gaps; MIT.
- SQL tuning: a real engine in the browser (PGlite); assert plan shape and buffers, not wall-clock (U); PEV2 plan viewer (PostgreSQL licence) [P]. About $0; no locking or concurrency labs.
- REST/back-end: a seeded discrete-event simulation of latency, cache and queue for reproducible scoring (U); indie browser simulators exist but are unverified [S]. About $0; model realism; own code.
- ML evaluation: real Pyodide plus scikit-learn for leakage and threshold labs (U). About $0 plus 31 MB first load; no torch; MPL-2.0.
- Testing and mutation: pytest in Pyodide with a custom mini-mutator (U); Stryker (Apache-2.0) needs Node, so server side; Code Defenders (LGPL-3.0, Tomcat plus MySQL) used in university courses: a pedagogy reference only [P/S].
- Secure coding: server targets for exploits (MultiJuicer) plus in-browser patch drills graded by exploit and regression tests (U); evidence for labs over simulation is weak [S, 2020, second-hand]. Under $0.01/h; highest abuse risk.
- Incident response: pure simulation, a scripted event timeline with synthetic dashboards and logs; Google's Wheel of Misfortune is simulated [S, 2021]; for a real runtime use the OpenTelemetry Demo failure flags [P]. About $0; authoring effort.
- Linux/DevOps: a server microVM (SaaS or Firecracker-based) plus a simulated shell for basics (U). $0.05 to $0.17/h; egress abuse. Gap: no authoritative practitioner comparison for REST design, SQL tuning, mutation testing or secure coding; those rows are inference.

**D. Grading without an LLM.** Instruqt check scripts fail on a non-zero exit and use `fail-message`; `track test` runs setup, check (must fail), solve, check (must pass) and can run in CI [P]; Labs 2.0 uses `condition` blocks with a 30 s default timeout [P]; Killercoda's `verify.sh` passes on exit 0 [P]. nbgrader (BSD-3) strips `### BEGIN HIDDEN TESTS` blocks from the student copy and restores them at grading, with checksum tamper detection [P]; pythonwhat and sqlwhat are AGPL-3.0 and stale. GitHub Skills steps are Actions workflows triggered by push with path filters [P]. xAPI (Apache-2.0 spec) is a candidate export format [P].

**Overall (U).** A browser-first runtime with three engine adapters (a JS simulator, Pyodide/PGlite, an optional microVM) that share one checker contract (state plus event log in; pass, score and feedback out); every simulation ships a solver and CI proves it fails at the start and passes when solved; skip WebContainers and CheerpX unless you accept a vendor quote. **Q4. Documented architectures for a tenant-agnostic runtime.** The pattern is the same across projects: each unit is declared by a manifest and sandboxed (iframe or web component), talks to the host through a typed state and event contract, and LMS standards are handled outside the unit.
- Concord: Activity Player loads activity JSON from the LARA authoring API; each interactive is an iframe using `@concord-consortium/lara-interactive-api` v1.15.0 (MIT, built on `iframe-phone`) with typed InteractiveState, AuthoredState, DialogState and global state, plus `setHeight`, `setHint`, `getAuthInfo` and React hooks.
- PhET-iO: the host page embeds the sim in an iframe and calls `phetioClient.invoke(phetioID, method, args, cb)`; state is JSON keyed by phetioID; events form a hierarchical stream of user, model and wrapper events; versions are pinned `MAJOR.MINOR` and API changes are opt-in; Studio exports a customised launch page; the `phetsims/phet-io` repo returns 404 (not public).
- OLI Torus: each activity type has a `manifest.json` declaring `delivery`, `authoring` and `preview` custom elements with entry files, plus `schema.ts` and `transformations`. Oppia: `extensions/` holds plugin directories (40 interactions, rich-text components, visualisations, answer summarisers, value generators). Open edX XBlock: plugins load through Python entry points; field scopes are content, settings, user_state, preferences, user_info and user_state_summary; per-block iframes "grind to a halt" past about 10 blocks, so the Learning MFE iframes the whole unit for cookie isolation (2024-07-26).
- Standards: LTI 1.3 is OAuth2 plus JWT with Deep Linking 2.0, AGS and NRPS 2.0; cmi5 launch parameters are `endpoint`, `fetch`, `actor`, `registration` and `activityId`, its statements are launched, initialized, completed, passed, failed, terminated, abandoned, waived and satisfied, it has `moveOn` rules and is a profile of xAPI 1.0.3; xAPI 2.0 is IEEE 9274.1.1-2023 [S].
- Inferences: a workable contract is a manifest (id, version, entry, state schema), one sandboxed iframe per lesson step rather than per widget, and a typed message API (init, setState, emit, complete, hint); events map to cmi5/xAPI statements with platform-side adapters for LTI grade passback; scoring uses a server-side check function as in Instruqt and Killercoda. Gaps: no public doc of XBlock sandbox attributes; no permissive open-source AI-simulation generator found; Concord's multi-tenant model undocumented.

**4. Optimisation pipeline.** glTF 2.0 with meshopt and KTX2, gated by the validator in CI; the tooling is permissive and actively maintained.
- Licences and activity [P]: glTF-Transform MIT, pushed 20 Oct 2026, CLI 4.5.0; gltfpack/meshoptimizer MIT with `-c`/`-cc` meshopt, `-tc` KTX2, `-si` simplify and `-mi` instancing (no Draco support); Draco, Basis Universal and glTF-Validator (web and npm) Apache-2.0; KTX-Software Apache-2.0 plus third-party components.
- three.js r186 (24 Sep 2026) loads Draco, EXT and KHR meshopt, KTX2, instancing and WebP [P]. KHR_meshopt_compression is still "Release Candidate", so use EXT_ for now [P]. meshoptimizer says Draco trades vertex order and decode speed for ratio [P].
- GPU memory: RGBA8 is 32 bpp, BC7/ASTC/UASTC are 8 bpp (4x smaller) and ETC1S is 4 bpp (8x); use ETC1S for colour and UASTC for normals (Khronos KTX guide) [P]. Shopify's checklist (undated): GLB about 4 MB, mobile-web textures 2048 or smaller, PBR, no triangle limit [P]; blog draw-call and triangle budgets are opinion [U]. AI output size: Tripo allows up to 1.5 to 2M faces and 8K textures; the TRELLIS.2 README sample uses 1M faces and 4096 textures [P].
- Recommended pipeline (inference): (1) choose a tier per asset: parametric code (own IP), CC0 or CC BY library, paid-API AI, or a commissioned artist for hero assets; (2) add a provenance sidecar (source, licence, tool, version, prompt, reviewer) and render CC BY credits in the app; (3) normalise to metres, Y-up, named selectable parts mapped to ontology or part ids; (4) optimise with `gltf-transform optimize` or gltfpack using meshopt and KTX2; (5) CI gates: validator errors, size and triangle checks, screenshot diff; (6) expert sign-off, then publish content-hashed files to S3. Starting budgets (the researcher's own judgement): phone at most 100k triangles, 100 draw calls, 2K textures and 5 MB per model with lazy loading; low-end laptop about 3x that; measure on real devices. Gap: no authoritative budget standard.

**5. Accuracy review.** Adopt PhET-style expert gating, label from ontologies, and review LLM-written text as strictly as the meshes.
- PhET design process (2009) [P]: a team of 3 to 5 including a scientist content expert, a teacher and an education researcher; goals come from literature on conceptual difficulties; a sim is posted only when the team finds it "clear, accurate and engaging"; interviews with target-background students and classroom use come before the "checkmark". Frontiers retracted (16 Feb 2024) a paper with AI-generated figures [P]. NIH 3D disclaims accuracy; users must verify [P].
- Label sources [P]: Cell Ontology CC BY 4.0; Gene Ontology CC BY 4.0 (asks for the release date); Uberon CC BY 3.0; BioNumbers states no licence (cite Milo et al. 2010 and the 6-digit ID).
- Inferences: give each asset a reference sheet (required structures, sizes, ontology ids); a subject-matter expert signs the mesh and its text against it; run think-aloud sessions with target learners; keep a versioned asset card re-reviewed on change, with a learner "report error" link; for IT topics anchor labels to standards and datasheets (EIA-310, vendor docs; licences not checked). Gap: no 2025 to 2026 error-rate study for AI-generated 3D teaching assets.

### D-ENGINES. Real-time 3D engines for browser learning simulations (researcher 1; state at 2026-10-26)

**1. Engine comparison.** three.js (with React Three Fiber and drei) and Babylon.js 9 are the only options that combine permissive licences, active releases, a WebGL2 baseline with optional WebGPU and a workable React path; PlayCanvas is a close third; Unity, Godot, Unreal, Wonderland and Needle each fail on licence cost, web runtime or workflow fit; no copyleft or non-commercial terms were found in the top three.

**2. LLM code-generation fitness.** three.js and R3F have by far the deepest LLM footprint and official LLM docs, yet free-form 3D generation is still unreliable; PlayCanvas has the most first-party agent tooling.
- Footprint: npm weekly downloads are three 15.2M, R3F 4.25M and drei 2.76M against @babylonjs/core 235K and playcanvas 99.6K [2]; Stack Overflow tags three.js 20,863, react-three-fiber 1,021, babylonjs 465, playcanvas 19 (Babylon and PlayCanvas use their own forums, so SO undercounts them) [38].
- Official LLM docs: three.js has docs/llms.txt (5 KB) and llms-full (363 KB) [3]; R3F has llms-full (175 KB) [7]; Babylon has a 4 KB llms.txt only (llms-full returns 404) [1]; PlayCanvas has an "entry point for AI agents", a 3.18 MB llms-full, `npx skills add playcanvas/skills` and an Editor MCP server (MIT), and warns that Engine 1 code "often no longer works" [17].
- Benchmarks: WorldCoder-Bench (2026-06-01) has 2,026 tasks; across nine frontier models the best score is 27.8% on core and 19.9% on robust; failures are state drift and broken interaction chains; whether it is three.js-only is unconfirmed [32]. 3JSBench (2026-10-09, three.js) publishes no scores and its author runs a commercial platform (U) [33]. - Inference: automated generation should target a typed Sim Spec plus a vetted component kit rather than arbitrary engine code; R3F's declarative JSX suits that best; PlayCanvas's Editor MCP is the only first-party agent loop but ties you to the cloud Editor. Gap: no controlled cross-engine LLM comparison.

**4. Education, accessibility, low-end.** PhET uses its own 2D scene graph, Scenery (MIT), with 3D added through three.js in `mobius` (pushed 2026-09-13) [35]. **5. Ranked recommendation.** (1) three.js + R3F + drei (MIT): the React-native model, React 19 peers, the smallest base weight, the deepest LLM and ecosystem footprint and official LLM docs; risks: you assemble the "engine" yourself, WebGPU APIs churn and official R3F WebGPU is still v10 alpha, the 2026 release cadence is slower, accessibility is DIY. (3) PlayCanvas: choose it if non-engineers must author scenes collaboratively and the roughly $50 per seat a month cloud lock-in is acceptable; its React wrapper is pre-1.0. For game-like feel with the best visuals and a small React/TypeScript team: R3F. For AI-automated generation: R3F with a constrained Sim Spec; PlayCanvas is the runner-up for vendor agent tooling. Architecture note: several of the eight topics (Git, testing, incidents, sales pipeline) are 2D diagrams, and PhET is mostly 2D; make 3D one renderer behind the Sim Spec and consider PixiJS 8 for 2D game-like sims. Gaps: real tree-shaken bundle sizes need a build (not run); prices for Unity Industry and Needle Pro; the Havok binary licence text; Epic's primary terms (403); Next 16-specific R3F reports.

### D-LEARNING. When 3D, immersion and gamification help or hurt, and the design and accessibility rules that follow (researcher 6; as of 26 Oct 2026)

Tags: [P] primary text read (paper abstract or text, standard, vendor docs); [S] secondary summary; [U] unverified or inference. Most learning-science evidence dates from 2005 to 2020; 2025 to 2026 items are labelled. DOI check: every DOI cited resolves in Crossref. Four DOIs first recalled were wrong (Keehner 2008, Krüger 2022, Plass 2014, Lancaster 2013); Keehner and Krüger were corrected through Crossref title search and Plass and Lancaster are not cited. Adams et al. 2008 (JILR) and the D'Angelo 2014 SRI report have no DOI; the arXiv paper resolves via DataCite only.

**1. When 3D, immersion and game layers help or hurt.** No evidence was found that more realism or immersion adds learning. 3D helps for spatial relations and procedural-motor content when the camera shows task-relevant views; it is neutral or harmful for conceptual content, through extra load and seductive detail; schematic visuals match or beat realistic ones in game meta-analyses.
- Makransky 2019 (doi:10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.12.007), headset lab simulation against desktop, N=52: presence d=1.30 higher, learning d=0.80 lower, EEG load d=0.59 higher [S]. Parong and Mayer 2018 (doi:10.1037/edu0000241): a slideshow beat VR on the posttest and VR was rated more engaging; segmenting plus summaries helped VR; no effect size retrieved [P]. Makransky and Mayer 2019 to 2021 (doi:10.1037/edu0000473), N=131 and 165: immersive VR against video gave more presence and enjoyment and no learning gain; enactment raised procedural (ηp² .144) and transfer (.088) in immersive VR only [P].
- Headset meta-analyses [P/S]: Wu 2020 (doi:10.1111/bjet.13023, 35 studies) ES 0.24 against non-immersive; Coban 2022 (doi:10.1016/j.edurev.2022.100452, 48 studies) g 0.38 overall and 0.30 against computer-assisted learning; a 2025 teacher-education meta-analysis (doi:10.3389/frvir.2025.1620905, 22 studies) g 0.52 with headsets 0.65 against desktop 0.18 (I²=72%, classroom and social skills). A 2026 field study (Computers in Human Behavior, doi:10.1016/j.chb.2025.108853) found immersive VR gave higher load and lower learning (abstract snippet only) [S].
- 3D against other methods [P]: Yammine 2015 (doi:10.1002/ase.1510, 36 studies) spatial d 0.50, factual 0.30; Krüger 2022 (doi:10.1016/j.caeo.2021.100065, N=150) AR 3D beat 2D for spatial relations with higher germane load and high mental-rotation learners gained more; Höffler and Leutner 2007 (doi:10.1016/j.learninstruc.2007.09.013) animation against static d 0.37, realistic 0.76, procedural-motor 1.06. Keehner 2008 (doi:10.1080/03640210801898177): free rotation did not help by itself; seeing task-relevant views did, whether active or passive.
- Seductive details [P]: Harp and Mayer 1998 (doi:10.1037/0022-0663.90.3.414); Rey 2012 (doi:10.1016/j.edurev.2012.05.003, 39 effects): small-to-medium harm to retention and medium to transfer; Wong and Adesope 2021 (doi:10.1007/s10648-020-09545-x): integral emotional design helped (retention g+ .35, transfer .27).
- Games [P]: Clark 2016 (doi:10.3102/0034654315582065, via the SRI summary): g 0.33 (k=57), schematic beat cartoon and realistic games, realism plus narrative had a small negative relation to learning, multi-session gains tied to spacing; Wouters 2013 (doi:10.1037/a0031311): learning d .29, retention .36, motivation .26 (n.s.), better when supplemented, multi-session or group play.
- Simulations [P]: D'Angelo 2014 (SRI brief, 59 K-12 studies 1991 to 2012): simulation against none g+ .62, enhanced against plain simulation .49; Chernikova 2020 (doi:10.3102/0034654320933544, 145 studies): g .85, scaffolds add, low prior knowledge benefits from examples and high from reflection; Smetana and Bell 2012 (doi:10.1080/09500693.2011.605182, 61 studies): best as a supplement with support and reflection; Rutten 2012 (doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2011.07.017) and de Jong 2013 (doi:10.1126/science.1230579): reviews with no pooled ES; simulations enhance instruction, especially labs, and virtual and physical should be combined.
- Gamification [P]: Sailer and Homner 2020 (doi:10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w) g .49 cognitive, .36 motivational, .25 behavioural (only cognitive stable); a 2026 higher-education meta-analysis (doi:10.1177/21582440261421375) d 1.12 but I²=98.8%, so low weight; Eger 2025 (doi:10.1080/1359432x.2024.2412360), N=355 employees: higher gamification intensity lowered autonomy and outcomes.
- Inference [U]: reserve 3D for spatial goals; use schematic 2D for abstract IT content such as pipelines, Git graphs and incident timelines; provide curated camera presets and linked 2D views; add self-explanation prompts; keep the game layer to feedback and progress, optional, with no narrative padding. Gaps: no study of desktop 3D against 2D on abstract IT topics and no 2025 desktop-3D meta-analysis; Höffler 2010 and Merchant 2014 abstracts not retrieved; Mayer's coherence effect-size table was paywalled.

**2. PhET.** Its gains come from intuitive direct manipulation, minimal text and help, implicit scaffolding and repeated interviews with target users. The evidence is physics and chemistry at K-12 and undergraduate level (2005 to 2014); no adult-workplace PhET studies were found.
- Process [P] (design process paper, 2009; research page): a team of 3 to 5 (developer, scientist, teacher, education researcher); learning goals first; a sim stays "under construction" until interviews show engagement and learning, then goes to classroom trial; 2 to 4 formal studies a year; each sim gets 4 to 6 think-aloud interviews; the 2008 corpus held over 200 interviews and over 60 sims (Adams 2008 Part II).
- Principles [P]: the sim must work without instruction or attention goes to the interface; students manipulate play-area objects first (about 10 minutes before touching the control panel); use an explicit visual model with cartoon-like real-world cues that "break" sensibly; novices weigh all visual cues equally, so remove irrelevant ones; start-up state should be minimal (an impressive Radio Waves start overwhelmed students); avoid help, and if needed keep it brief, on request and beside the item. Implicit scaffolding [P] (Podolefsky 2013, arXiv 1306.6544): affordances, constraints, cueing and feedback "guide without students feeling guided".
- Outcomes [P]: Finkelstein 2005 (doi:10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.1.010103): simulated circuits beat real equipment on concepts and on assembling a real circuit; Chamberlain 2014 (doi:10.1039/C4RP00009A), n=210: exploratory-feature use was 85% under light, 68% under moderate and 9% under heavy guidance; Moore 2013 (doi:10.1039/C3RP20157K), n=80, no instructions: students used 18 of 23 features; Lazonder and Harmsen 2016 (doi:10.3102/0034654315627366), 72 studies: guidance d=.50; PhET notes students will not explore without an incentive.
- Replicable [U]: all the design rules, plus about 5 think-aloud sessions per sim with target employees; the classroom validation history cannot be replicated. Licence: PhET HTML sims published before 29 Mar 2026 are CC BY 4.0, which permits commercial use with attribution and logo; terms for newer sims could not be retrieved [U].

**3. Workplace learners.** Simulations work best as active, repeatable, feedback-rich supplements; entertainment value adds little; effects shrink at higher Kirkpatrick levels, so measure beyond reaction.
- Sitzmann 2011 (doi:10.1111/j.1744-6570.2011.01190.x) [P]: 65 studies, N=6,476; self-efficacy 20% higher, declarative 11%, procedural 14%, retention 9%; strong publication bias; gains larger when content was delivered actively, replays were unlimited and the simulation supplemented other instruction; simulations lost to an active comparison.
- Kirkpatrick-level evidence [P]: Arthur 2003 (doi:10.1037/0021-9010.88.2.234) d≈.60 for reaction (k=15), learning (k=234), behaviour (k=122) and results (k=26); Alliger 1997 (doi:10.1111/j.1744-6570.1997.tb00911.x): "useful for my job" reactions predict transfer better than enjoyment; Cook 2011 (doi:10.1001/jama.2011.1234; health professions, 609 studies): knowledge 1.20, behaviours about 0.8, patient outcomes 0.50.
- Design features for skills outcomes [P] (Cook 2013, doi:10.3109/0142159X.2012.714886): range of difficulty .68, repetitive practice .68, distributed practice .66, interactivity .65, multiple strategies .62, feedback .44. Feedback [P]: Gegenfurtner 2014 (doi:10.1111/bjet.12188, k=15): more user control helped, assessment feedback after training beat feedback during it, social, narrative and multimedia features were not significant; van der Kleij 2015 (doi:10.3102/0034654314564881): elaborated feedback .49, correct answer .32, right/wrong .05, delay hurt on average.
- Realism [P]: Norman 2012 (doi:10.1111/j.1365-2923.2012.04243.x, 24 studies): high-fidelity advantage only 1 to 2%; Hamstra 2014 (doi:10.1097/ACM.0000000000000130) argues for functional task alignment over physical resemblance. Transfer [P]: Blume 2010 (doi:10.1177/0149206309352880, 89 studies): predictors are ability, conscientiousness, motivation and a supportive work environment; effects stronger for open skills such as communication than closed skills such as software. Session length [P]: Donovan 1999 (doi:10.1037/0021-9010.84.5.795) spaced beat massed (d=.46); a 2025 microlearning review (doi:10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e41413, 40 studies, segments of about 1 to 15 minutes) was positive but gave no pooled ES. Gaps: no direct evidence on optimal session length; no 2024 to 2026 serious-game meta-analysis for employees; Kirkpatrick Partners' site was blocked.

**4. Accessibility.**
- WCAG 2.2 is the W3C Recommendation (this text dated 12 Dec 2024; first published 5 Oct 2023); WCAG 3.0 is still a Working Draft (10 Sep 2026), so do not target it [P]. EN 301 549 V3.2.1 (2021) is in force and based on WCAG 2.1; V4.1.1 was published 2 Sep 2026 and adopts WCAG 2.2 A/AA (Deque, 8 Sep 2026; DWT, 18 Sep 2026) [S]; a presumption of conformity needs citation in the EU Official Journal, which Deque forecasts for about 30 Nov 2026 (an earlier snippet said 16 Dec 2026, unverified); the ETSI PDF was not reachable.
- Success-criteria checklist for 3D and canvas [P]: 1.1.1 text alternatives, 4.1.2 name/role/value, 2.1.1 keyboard, 2.1.2 no trap (all A); 2.4.7 focus visible and 2.4.11 focus not obscured (AA); 2.5.1 pointer gestures (A); 2.5.7 dragging alternative and 2.5.8 24×24 px targets (AA); 1.4.1 colour, 1.4.3 contrast 4.5:1, 1.4.11 contrast 3:1 for graphics, 1.4.4 and 1.4.10 resize and reflow; 2.2.1 timing, 2.2.2 pause moving content over 5 seconds, 2.3.1 three flashes; 2.3.3 disabling interaction-triggered motion (Level AAA, so optional under EN 301 549); 1.2.2 captions, 1.2.5 audio description, 4.1.3 status messages (for AI chat and feedback); `prefers-reduced-motion` has been Baseline widely available since Jan 2020 [P].
- W3C XR requirements [P]: the XAUR note (25 Aug 2021, not baseline requirements) asks for object query, mutable animations, large targets, adjustable speed, no seizure triggers and text for audio. PhET reference implementation [P]: a parallel DOM inside a custom scene graph gives keyboard and screen-reader access (W4A 2018, doi:10.1145/3192714.3192817) and uses an auditory-description framework (CHI 2020, doi:10.1145/3313831.3376460); its features are alternative input, interactive description, sonification and voicing (the feature list is [S] because the PhET accessibility page did not render). Babylon.js [P]: the accessibility package builds an HTML twin of tagged objects, supports only three pick triggers and says to tag only what matters; a forum report of lag with thousands of meshes is [S]. Typical WebGL failures [S] (three.js forum practitioners, Sep to Oct 2025): the canvas carries no semantics and a twin hidden with `display:none` is invisible to screen readers; fixes they suggest are a DOM mirror, a "warp" keyboard mode, live announcements and testing with NVDA; mapping these failures to specific success criteria is the researcher's inference [U].

**5. Performance and devices.** Global support [P]: WebGL 97.5%, WebGL2 97.1%, WebGPU 85.8% plus 3.7% partial (caniuse, fetched 26 Oct 2026); WebGPU ships in Chrome and Edge 113+, Firefox 141 (Windows) and 145 (macOS ARM) and Safari 26; Linux, Intel Mac and Android support is partial (web.dev, 25 Nov 2025). Budgets [P]: Core Web Vitals (page updated 31 Oct 2024) LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms, CLS ≤0.1 at the 75th percentile. Adaptive tiers [P]: detect-gpu (MIT): tier 0 is no WebGL, blocklisted or under 15 fps, with a non-WebGL fallback; tiers 1 to 3 are ≥15, ≥30 and ≥60 fps; its GFXBench data stopped updating in Dec 2025. Babylon's SceneOptimizer targets 60 fps and re-checks every 2 s, degrading in this order: merge meshes and shadows, then post-processes and particles, then textures, then render targets, then hardware scaling. Asset budgets [P]: Khronos v1.0 (Oct 2020) sets, for a desktop 3D web view, 3 MB, 250k triangles, under 100 draw calls and 2K textures, and for a per-item planning tool 1 MB, 40k triangles and under 5 draw calls (product-asset targets, not whole-scene budgets, U); Khronos 2.0 (7 Aug 2025) gives no numbers and recommends Draco and MeshOpt compression. Fleet data [S]: no 2025 to 2026 data on the low-end share of corporate fleets; the only proxy is that about 40% of Windows PCs still ran Windows 10 in Sep 2025 (StatCounter via Gigazine, 15 Oct 2025); no memory-budget source found. Inference [U]: take an integrated GPU as the baseline, target 60 fps with automatic drop to 30, cap device pixel ratio, lazy-load assets and fall back to 2D or SVG on tier 0; measure each tenant's real fleet with telemetry.
