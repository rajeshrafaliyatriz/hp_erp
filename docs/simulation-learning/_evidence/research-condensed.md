# Condensed reading of the six research notes

Saved 2026-10-26 from the approved plan (the whole plan is in `_evidence/approved-plan-2026-10-26.md`). Claims are tagged verified, inferred or unverified in the source text.


**E-1. 3D engines (done). Shortlist for the spike: three.js with React Three Fiber and drei, and Babylon.js 9; PlayCanvas only if non-engineers must author scenes together; a 2D control.**
- *Ranking by the researcher, with reasons.* (1) **three.js r186 + R3F 9.8.2 + drei 10.7.9 (MIT):** the React-native model (R3F peers `react >=19 <19.4`, matching g2gv0), the smallest base weight, by far the deepest LLM and ecosystem footprint (three 15.2M npm downloads a week against Babylon's 235K and PlayCanvas's 99.6K) and official LLM docs. Risks: you assemble the "engine" yourself (physics, state, UI), WebGPU APIs still churn (breaking changes in 7 of the last 9 migration-guide sections) and R3F's official WebGPU is v10 alpha, accessibility is do-it-yourself. (2) **Babylon.js 9.32 (Apache-2.0):** batteries included (Havok physics, GUI, Inspector, Node Material Editor, Playground, Babylon Editor, SceneOptimizer, a hidden-DOM accessibility twin of the scene, WebGPU parity). Risks: a large bundle, a small React wrapper (react-babylonjs 4.0.2), a weaker LLM footprint (its maintainers say assistants invent Babylon APIs). (3) **PlayCanvas 2.23 (engine MIT):** strong first-party agent tooling (llms-full, skills, an Editor MCP server) and a collaborative cloud Editor, but the Editor is a paid cloud service ($15 a month; $50 per seat a month for an organisation; the free plan is public projects only) and its React wrapper is pre-1.0.
- *Rejected on licence cost or web fit.* Unity 6 (runtime fee cancelled in Sep 2024, but non-game firms with over $1M finances need Industry at a custom price; web defaults to WebGL2, WebGPU "limited"; React only through a community wrapper); Godot 4.7 (MIT, but web is WebGL2 only, a stock export is about 42 MB, accessibility partial); Unreal Pixel Streaming (one GPU per session, about $0.53 an hour on AWS for 1 to 4 sessions); Wonderland (proprietary, 10% royalty above $120K); Needle (paid Pro licence for commercial use); A-Frame (MIT, three-based); Cesium and deck.gl (geospatial only). For 2D game-like simulations PixiJS 8 (MIT, WebGL + WebGPU, 237 KB gzip) is the option.
- *Measured download sizes (gzip; the researcher's figures and mine agree within about 3%).* three core 190 to 194 KB; three WebGPU build 309 to 400 KB; R3F 58 KB; drei is large but tree-shakeable (whole package 521 KB); Babylon UMD 1.84 to 1.89 MB; PlayCanvas 640 to 656 KB; physics is heavy: Rapier about 1.1 to 1.6 MB, Havok 643 to 658 KB, so lazy-load physics only for simulations that need it.
- *Performance and quality evidence is thin.* No independent cross-engine benchmark exists (only a vendor-run one and a single-user forum post in which three.js's WebGPURenderer was twice as slow on the CPU and 5 to 10 times slower on the first frame than WebGLRenderer on 4,000 meshes, r183); visual ceilings look comparable (Babylon 9 has clustered and volumetric lights, PlayCanvas has CameraFrame, three.js has TSL post-processing nodes and a path tracer). No low-end integrated-GPU or phone measurements exist for any engine, so the spike must produce them. **Baseline WebGL2 (97.1% support); WebGPU progressive (85.8% support; Chrome and Edge 113+, Android 12+ on Qualcomm and ARM GPUs, Safari and iOS 26, Firefox 141 on Windows).**
- *AI generation fitness.* No controlled cross-engine comparison exists. The best of nine frontier models scored 27.8% on the core tier of WorldCoder-Bench (Jun 2026; failures were state drift and broken interaction chains). So generation should target a typed Sim Spec plus a vetted component kit, not arbitrary engine code; R3F's declarative JSX suits that best, and PlayCanvas's Editor MCP is the only first-party agent loop (but it ties you to the cloud Editor).
- *Architecture and accessibility notes.* Several of the topics (Git, testing, incidents, sales pipeline) are 2D diagrams and PhET is mostly 2D, so make 3D one renderer behind the Sim Spec. Canvas content is opaque to assistive technology in every engine: keep essential content and controls in the DOM (code and data panels, labels) and treat 3D as a visual layer; Babylon offers the most built-in help. Adaptive quality: R3F has demand rendering and PerformanceMonitor, Babylon has SceneOptimizer. PhET itself uses its own 2D scene graph (Scenery, MIT) and adds three.js only in a few sims; Labster uses Unity (vendor page).
**E-2. 3D assets and AI 3D generation (done).**
- *Two routes to a polished scene, and the evidence says the cheaper one works.* Learn Arduino (Appendix A9) shows that code-built geometry with good lighting needs about 155 KB and no asset licences. The cell explorers mix code-built shapes with a few GLB models: 9 of the 12 cells in the 8gwifi atlas are procedural and 3 use NIH 3D models; the MIT clone of the Cell Architecture Studio ships two user-supplied GLBs of about 59 and 57 MB. My first recommendation here (procedural by default) was rejected by the owner; the policy is now downloaded models, restyled into one look (section 5c, item 2), each with a provenance sidecar (source, licence, tool, version, prompt, reviewer).
- *AI 3D generators: the real licence texts were read.* TRELLIS.2 is MIT, but its GLB export depends on NVIDIA nvdiffrast (research and evaluation only, "not for any direct or indirect monetary gain") and its default pipeline loads RMBG-2.0 (CC BY-NC 4.0), and it needs at least 24 GB of VRAM, so it is not safe to self-host commercially. Hunyuan3D 2.1 grants rights "for the Territory only", which excludes the EU, UK and South Korea, and bars using its output outside it: unsafe for a multi-tenant SaaS. Meshy: free tier is CC BY 4.0 and public, paid tiers are private and customer-owned, API from Pro. Tripo: free tier, Tripo keeps all rights; paid users "generally have all rights" and inputs are not used for training. Rodin: commercial export depends on the plan, API on the $120 a month Business tier. Stable Fast 3D is commercial only under US$1M revenue with registration; SAM 3D Objects has no commercial bar. **Use paid-tier outputs only.** The US Copyright Office (Jan 2025) found prompts alone are not authorship, so AI meshes may be unprotectable: add human edits. There is no independent test on organelles, racks or tools; rankings are vendor-run or reward looks (voters gave textured models +144 Elo).
- *Free asset libraries, licence-safe.* CC0: Poly Haven (521 models; none are servers or network gear), Kenney, Quaternius. Sketchfab is per model (CC BY needs credit; avoid Standard and Editorial licences, which forbid extraction; owned by KitBash since Aug 2026). NIH 3D is per entry (many public domain or CC BY, some more restrictive, accuracy not guaranteed; the 8gwifi atlas shows CC BY-NC-SA entries are in use). Khronos sample assets are feature tests only. Smithsonian is CC0 where marked. Avoid Allen Cell (non-commercial) and treat Z-Anatomy and BodyParts3D (CC BY-SA) as copyleft. No clean CC0 library of servers or network gear exists: build racks in code (1U is 44.45 mm, EIA-310), possibly driven by Blender MCP (MIT).
- *Optimisation pipeline.* glTF 2.0 with meshopt (use the EXT extension; the KHR one is still a release candidate) and KTX2 textures (ETC1S for colour, UASTC for normals), gated by glTF-Validator in CI; tools are glTF-Transform (MIT), gltfpack/meshoptimizer (MIT), Draco, Basis Universal, KTX-Software and glTF-Validator (Apache-2.0); three r186 loads all of them. Starting budgets are the researcher's judgement, since no authoritative standard exists: phone at most 100k triangles, 100 draw calls, 2K textures and 5 MB per model; low-end laptop about three times that. Raw GLBs from AI flows are 50 MB or more, so optimising is mandatory.
- *Accuracy review.* PhET's process (a team of 3 to 5 with a scientist, a teacher and an education researcher; student interviews before a sim gets its "checkmark") is the model. Give each asset a reference sheet (structures, sizes, ontology ids), have an expert sign the mesh and any LLM-written text, keep a versioned asset card with a learner "report error" link, and take labels from Cell Ontology (CC BY 4.0), Gene Ontology (CC BY 4.0) or Uberon (CC BY 3.0); BioNumbers states no licence (cite Milo et al. 2010 and the id). A 2024 Frontiers paper with AI-generated figures was retracted. Gap: no 2025 to 2026 study of error rates in AI-generated teaching 3D.
**E-3. Similar products and open-source repos (done).**
- *The market:* no product is both commercially reusable and LMS-complete. PhET is withdrawing code (its polyrepos were "sunset" on 9 Oct 2026) and tightening its licence; GeoGebra's binaries and materials are non-commercial (commercial use needs a special licence); Labster (WebGL 3D, Chrome 114+, no Safari; LTI 1.3 with Deep Linking and grade services), PraxiLabs and Gizmos (LTI sign-in only, no grade passback) are closed SaaS; Wokwi (browser Arduino) sells per seat (€0 to €20 a month) with no LMS integration. The 2025 to 2026 newcomers are AI generators: Google "learning interactives" (17 Sep 2026: the teacher approves objectives, the system builds levels, hints and solutions, and an agent plays the simulation in Chrome to test that it is solvable, then a human reviews), and Gemini (24 Aug 2026) and ChatGPT (10 Mar 2026) in-chat simulations, which no tenant can control. **Google's pipeline (objectives, then levels, hints and solutions, then an automated play-test, then human review) is worth copying.**
- *Corporate and IT training:* IT labs (Instruqt, Killercoda, KodeKloud, TryHackMe, Hack The Box) use declarative content in git plus a pass/fail check script; soft-skill vendors (Mursion, Talespin (bought by Cornerstone in 2024), Bodyswaps, Gamelearn) compete on AI role-play and rubrics; Whatfix Mirror (sandbox replicas of an app plus AI role-play) and WalkMe Learning Arc (Demo, Practice and Test modes; one-way SCORM export) are nearest to "guided practice in a sandbox copy of the product". LTI is common but partial (Instruqt: resource link and grade service only, score always 1.0); SCORM and xAPI are rare. Copy WalkMe's three modes, the check-script pass/fail and author-defined rubrics; plan LTI 1.3 plus cmi5 for later.
- *Open-source building blocks (licence, stars and last push read from GitHub today).* PhET-style 2D: **SceneryStack** (MIT, npm 3.0.0, "multimodal, accessible HTML5 interactives", built from PhET's own MIT libraries) is the lawful route to PhET-style tooling and goes into the spike as the 2D candidate. Runtimes: Concord activity-player and lara (MIT), Oppia (Apache-2.0, 6.9k stars), OLI Torus (MIT), Open edX XBlock (Apache-2.0). Learning games and visualisers: learnGitBranching (MIT, 34k), TensorFlow Playground (Apache-2.0), transformer-explainer (MIT, 9k), sql-mysteries and cmdchallenge (MIT), oh-my-git (Blue Oak 1.0.0; check its art), Juice Shop (MIT). 3D: Mol*, 3Dmol.js, NGL, vtk-js, three.js, R3F, Babylon.js, PlayCanvas, Godot, matter-js, Rapier. Circuits, narrative and tracking: CircuitVerse (MIT), avr8js and wokwi-elements (MIT), ink and YarnSpinner (MIT), scorm-again (MIT), ltijs (Apache-2.0), packbackbooks/lti-1-3-php-library (Apache-2.0, PHP, fits Laravel). Copyleft, so do not fork into the product: circuitjs1, Digital, NetLogo, WebGoat, Twine, OSP/Tracker, Open edX platform (AGPL), Pyodide (MPL). Non-commercial or unlicensed, so no: GeoGebra, microsims (CC BY-NC), mathigon/studio media (CC BY-SA), OSP (no licence file), PhET sim repos, Z-Anatomy models (CC BY-SA).
- *Runtime architectures to read first.* They all share one shape: a manifest per unit, a sandboxed iframe or web component, a typed state-and-event contract, and LMS standards handled outside the unit. Concord's Activity Player with `lara-interactive-api` (typed interactive state, authored state, hints; MIT); PhET-iO (the host calls `invoke(phetioID, …)`, state is JSON keyed by id, events are a hierarchical stream, versions are pinned MAJOR.MINOR); OLI Torus (a `manifest.json` per activity type with delivery, authoring and preview elements); Oppia (plugin directories); Open edX XBlock (entry points and field scopes; per-block iframes slow the page past about 10, so the whole unit is framed). **Lesson: one sandboxed iframe per lesson step, not per widget.** Standards: LTI 1.3 (OAuth2 + JWT, Deep Linking 2.0, grade services), cmi5 (a profile of xAPI 1.0.3 with launched, passed, failed, completed and moveOn rules), xAPI 2.0 (IEEE 9274.1.1-2023). Gaps: no permissive open-source AI-simulation generator exists; Concord's multi-tenant model is undocumented.
**E-4. IT hands-on labs and sandboxes (done): simulate in the browser, or run a real sandbox?**
- *Pattern to adopt:* one runtime with three engine adapters (a JavaScript simulator, Pyodide/PGlite in the browser, an optional micro-VM) that all obey one checker contract: simulation state plus event log in, pass/score/feedback out. **Every simulation ships a solver, and CI proves its check fails at the start and passes once solved** (the Instruqt `track test` pattern). Browser-side grading can be tampered with, so re-run the hidden checks on the server for anything certification-grade. nbgrader-style hidden tests with tamper checksums are the model for code tasks.
- *In the browser:* Pyodide 314.0.7 (Python 3.14.2, Sep 2026; MPL-2.0) bundles scikit-learn 1.8, numpy, scipy, pandas and pytest but has no threads or real sockets, no torch, and costs about 31 MB cold (runtime 9.6, scipy 14.0, scikit-learn 4.4, numpy 3.0); stopping runaway code needs a worker plus SharedArrayBuffer headers. PGlite (Apache-2.0, about 3 MB gzipped) is a real Postgres for SQL and EXPLAIN labs (single user, no locking labs). JupyterLite (BSD-3), SQLite/DuckDB-WASM and xterm.js (MIT) are clean. WebContainers and CheerpX are proprietary and need a paid licence even for employee use (no public price): skip unless you accept a vendor quote. Sandpack is stale; Nodebox is internal or non-commercial only. v86 and container2wasm are toys.
- *Server side:* micro-VM sandboxes cost about $0.05 to $0.17 per learner-hour (Vercel Sandbox, Firecracker, 1 vCPU/2 GB; E2B similar; Fargate about $0.05), so 11 learners at 10 hours each is roughly $6 to $19 a month; the real costs are authoring, abuse control (egress deny-list, TTL, quotas, one VM per session) and operations. Play with Docker's hosted service ended 1 Mar 2026; CTFd checks only static flags; Juice Shop (MIT) with MultiJuicer (Apache-2.0) is under $0.01 an hour per learner.
- *Per topic:* Git: an in-browser model for concepts (learnGitBranching runs 100% client-side) plus isomorphic-git for real merge and conflict drills; isomorphic-git has no rebase, and neither has reflog, so "recover a lost commit" and rebase drills need our own engine or a server micro-VM; avoid wasm-git (GPLv2). SQL tuning: PGlite with plan-shape assertions (not wall-clock) and the PEV2 plan viewer. Back-end design: our own seeded discrete-event model (reproducible scoring). ML evaluation: our own JavaScript model for interactivity, with scikit-learn run in CI as the oracle; use Pyodide only when live training is essential (31 MB). Testing: pytest in Pyodide or a JS runner with a small mutator; Stryker needs Node (server side); Code Defenders (LGPL) as a pedagogy reference only. Secure coding: server targets (MultiJuicer) plus patch drills graded by exploit and regression tests; highest abuse risk, and evidence for labs over simulation is weak. Incident response: pure simulation (a scripted timeline with synthetic dashboards and logs), as Google's "Wheel of Misfortune". Linux/DevOps: server micro-VM plus a simulated shell for the basics.
- *Copyleft or restricted flags:* MPL-2.0 Pyodide and Hypothesis; AGPL-3.0 Coder, pythonwhat, sqlwhat; LGPL-3.0 Code Defenders; GPL WebGoat, DVWA, wasm-git; EPL-2.0 Eclipse Che; source-available Nodebox; proprietary WebContainers and CheerpX. Not found: authoritative practitioner comparisons for REST design, SQL tuning, mutation testing and secure coding (those rows are inference).

**E-5. AI-generated simulations and AI help (done).**
- *Three ways teams generate simulations.* (a) Free-form code: accuracy is poor. The best of 10 models passed 21.5% of 334 human-verified tests on SimuScene (Feb 2026); the best baseline passed 23.8% of physical assertions on PhysCodeBench (a corrector lifted it to 70.6%); on MooseBench, 39 to 40% of repaired cases ran but solved the wrong physics. (b) A declarative manifest over a trusted component catalog: Google A2UI (Apache-2.0, v0.8, "early"), Vercel Labs json-render (Apache-2.0), MCP Apps (sandboxed iframes with JSON-RPC over postMessage). (c) Tool-call edits to structured scene state: a 24-person study (Jul 2026) found users under-notice the AI's confabulations, so every AI edit must return the verified resulting state. **Decision: a validated, typed manifest is the default; reviewed code is the exception; check A2UI, json-render and MCP Apps for a format worth adopting before inventing one.** Gap: no benchmark exists for generated slider-style teaching simulations or IT-process simulations.
- *Validation gate order (proposed from the evidence).* Schema, then static checks (AST allow-list, units, ranges), then oracle / metamorphic / property tests, then a cross-solver differential test, then a headless render with an accessibility check, then expert sign-off, then an immutable versioned publish. "Runs without error" is a weak proxy; the effective checks read engine state or rebuild the model and compare it with the spec. Unsafe code is common: 45% of LLM outputs had OWASP Top-10 flaws (Veracode, Jul 2025) and 5.2% (commercial models) to 21.7% (open models) of suggested packages did not exist (USENIX Security 2025), so vendor every library and allow no runtime installs.
- *AI hints.* Scaffolded, content-grounded tutors help: Kestin et al. (Sci Rep, Jun 2025) 0.73 to 1.3 SD in intro physics with pre-written solutions given to the tutor; Bastani et al. (PNAS, Jul 2025) a guard-railed tutor raised practice scores 127% while plain chat fell 17% once removed. Without checks, 32% of ChatGPT hints failed quality review (Pardos and Bhandari 2024; self-consistency cut that to 0 to 13%). Khanmigo's two-year cluster trial (Aug 2026) found only 0.06 to 0.08 SD a year: engagement is the limit. Adversarial learners extract answers (small open tutors leaked 34 to 82%; reason-then-respond or a judge plus refiner cut it to 2 to 6%). Design: a hint ladder gated by simulation state, hints generated from the author's reference solution plus a state snapshot, a second-pass leakage check, a golden set that includes adversarial turns, and a human audit sample. Gap: no evidence yet for AI hints inside simulations specifically.
- *AI gateway.* Meter per tenant in our own database in addition to any gateway budget (Vercel AI Gateway offers budgets, fallbacks and logs). LiteLLM was compromised twice in 2026 (24 Mar and 21 Oct), so do not depend on it unpinned. OpenAI announced it is acquiring promptfoo (MIT) on 9 Mar 2026 and says it will keep the open-source project; ClickHouse acquired Langfuse on 16 Jan 2026 and says it stays open source (both confirmed from the companies' own announcements): watch their direction, or use Inspect AI (MIT) or DeepEval (Apache-2.0). Avoid LLM Guard (archived Jul 2026) and Semgrep's rules (licence bars bundling in a SaaS). Pyodide and axe-core are MPL-2.0 (file-level copyleft; fine unmodified). OWASP LLM Top 10 (2025): treat model output as untrusted (LLM05), cap consumption (LLM10).
- *Sandbox.* A dedicated cookie-less domain on object storage, `sandbox="allow-scripts"` only (with `allow-same-origin` on a same-origin embed the page can remove its own sandbox), a CSP, and `credentialless`. **A sandboxed frame reports origin "null", so the host must authenticate messages by `event.source` plus a per-session nonce, never by origin alone**, and must never post to `"*"`. Real incident: Open WebUI CVE-2026-87995 (CVSS 8.7) came from an iframe with both flags. Not researched: CPU and memory limits, Safari behaviour.

**E-6. Learning science, accessibility and performance (done).**
- *3D and game layers: the evidence tempers "best 3D".* Nothing shows that more realism or immersion adds learning. 3D helps spatial relations and procedural-motor content when the camera shows task-relevant views (3D beat other methods on spatial content, d 0.50; animation beat static for procedural-motor content, d 1.06; free rotation alone did not help). It is neutral or harmful for conceptual content, through extra load and seductive detail (a headset lab simulation gave more presence but less learning, d −0.80 on learning; a slideshow beat VR in another study). In games, schematic visuals matched or beat cartoon and realistic ones; gamification gains are small (g 0.49 cognitive, 0.25 behavioural), and a 355-employee study found intense gamification lowered autonomy and outcomes. **Rules: reserve 3D for spatial goals (system flow, structure, consequence); use schematic 2D for abstract IT content such as Git graphs, pipelines and timelines; stylised, not photoreal; curated camera presets plus a linked 2D view; self-explanation prompts; a game layer limited to feedback and progress, optional, with no narrative padding.** Gap: no study of desktop 3D against 2D on abstract IT topics.
- *Simulation effects and PhET's method.* Simulations beat no simulation (g 0.62 over 59 K-12 studies) and enhanced simulations beat plain ones (0.49); with scaffolds, simulation-based learning in higher education reached g 0.85; they work best as a supplement with support and reflection; guidance adds d 0.50. PhET's rules: it must work without instruction, learners manipulate the play area first, one explicit visual model, a minimal start-up state, little help, implicit scaffolding, and 4 to 6 think-aloud interviews per sim; learners will not explore without an incentive. Replicable here: all the rules, plus about 5 think-aloud sessions per simulation with target employees. No adult-workplace PhET studies exist.
- *Workplace learners.* Sitzmann (2011, 65 studies): self-efficacy +20%, declarative knowledge +11%, procedural +14%, retention +9%, larger when the simulation is active, replayable and a supplement (strong publication bias). Features that moved skills most (Cook 2013): range of difficulty and repetitive practice (0.68), distributed practice (0.66), interactivity (0.65), feedback (0.44); elaborated feedback beats right-or-wrong (0.49 against 0.05); fidelity adds only 1 to 2%, so match the task functionally, not physically. Measure beyond reaction: "useful for my job" predicts transfer better than enjoyment; transfer is stronger for open skills such as communication than closed ones such as software, and depends on a supportive workplace. Spaced beats massed practice (d 0.46). Gaps: no direct evidence on the best session length; no 2024 to 2026 serious-game meta-analysis for employees.
- *Accessibility target.* WCAG 2.2 AA (a W3C Recommendation; WCAG 3.0 is still a draft). EN 301 549 V3.2.1 is in force and V4.1.1 (published 2 Sep 2026) adopts WCAG 2.2 A/AA, with EU citation expected about 30 Nov 2026 (secondary sources). For 3D and canvas: keyboard operation and no trap (2.1.1, 2.1.2), name/role/value (4.1.2), text alternatives (1.1.1), focus visible and not obscured (2.4.7, 2.4.11), a dragging alternative and 24 px targets (2.5.7, 2.5.8), contrast 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for graphics (1.4.3, 1.4.11), pause for moving content over 5 seconds (2.2.2), no more than three flashes (2.3.1), status messages for AI chat (4.1.3), and `prefers-reduced-motion`. Reference implementations: PhET's parallel DOM inside its scene graph (interactive description, sonification, voicing) and Babylon's accessibility twin; a twin hidden with `display:none` is invisible to screen readers; test with NVDA.
- *Performance.* Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile: LCP up to 2.5 s, INP up to 200 ms, CLS up to 0.1. detect-gpu (MIT) gives tiers (0 = no WebGL or under 15 fps, then 15, 30 and 60 fps) but its benchmark data stopped updating in Dec 2025, so add a runtime frame-rate probe; Babylon's SceneOptimizer steps quality down to hold 60 fps. Khronos product-asset targets (2020) for a desktop web view are 3 MB, 250k triangles, under 100 draw calls and 2K textures (not whole-scene budgets). No data exists on the low-end share of corporate fleets (a proxy: about 40% of Windows PCs still ran Windows 10 in Sep 2025). Baseline: an integrated GPU, 60 fps with automatic drop to 30, a capped pixel ratio, lazy loading, a 2D or SVG fallback on tier 0, and telemetry of each tenant's real fleet.
