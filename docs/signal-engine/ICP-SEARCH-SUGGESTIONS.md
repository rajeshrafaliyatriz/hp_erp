# Search suggestions for the three ICPs (for owner review)

Nothing in this file is applied automatically. You review it, then edit the ICP in the Product Portfolio screen if you agree (last section explains how).

How to read the tables: "Topic" is the kind of search (news = recent news and notices, general = any web page such as a tender notice or a company page). "Days" is how far back to look. "Trigger" is the buying event from your ICP that the search is hunting for.

Evidence used: the three live ICP exports, the first market preview of each ICP (the "probe" files), and the source readability check (26 government and tender sources, 10 readable, 16 not).

A caution up front: I have not run any of the new searches. They are my best judgement of wording that should surface named Indian buyers. Expect some to work and some not; the first preview after you apply them will tell us which to keep.

## 1. G2G partners (B2C, companies that could resell or implement G2G)

### 1.1 What is weak today
- **Searches return categories, not companies.** Of the 64 searches, many are phrased as a category ("Government IT system integrators India", "corporate training company", "manufacturing"). The preview's five results included "System Integrators (Indian ICT channel ecosystem)", which is a whole industry, not a company. Only one entry was a named firm with a real signal.
- **Searches are too short or too wide, and some leak outside India.** "technical training provider", "HR transformation consulting firm" and "executive search firm manufacturing Ahmedabad" carry no India or buyer-type words. The preview pulled in overseas HR consultancies, recruitment firms, course sellers and e-learning vendors, and the AI step had to throw them away. Several results were from LinkedIn, which cannot be read, so they stayed as unverified leads.
- **Mixed-up targets.** The ICP lists IT integrators and also training and HR firms, but the search list does not separate them. A one-word search like "manufacturing" cannot find a partner. One preview result was a CCTV installer; another was an academic paper on a health-skills study.

### 1.3 Which sources fit
- Fit well but only as clue lists, not for proposing partners: **BidAssist** (readable; shows which companies bid and win), **GeM bids** and **CPPP** (readable; award and bidder details sometimes appear), **C-DAC** and **MyGov** (readable; list implementation agencies and programmes).
- Not usable: Tender247, TenderDetail, NIELIT (blocked by their rules), PIB and NIC (refuse automated reading).
- Honest view: for partners the best evidence is company news and company pages, not government portals. The portals only help to spot who is winning government work.

### 1.4 Rules to add (do not propose)
1. Opinion posts, blog explainers and "what is a competency framework" articles.
2. Research papers and academic journal pages (the preview offered one).
3. Companies headquartered outside India or serving only overseas markets, unless they have an Indian delivery team.
4. Industry categories or lists with no single named company (for example "Indian ICT channel ecosystem").
5. Hardware or security-installation firms (CCTV, networking, device resellers) with no software or consulting practice.

## 2. ScholarClone B2G (state education departments and missions)

### 2.1 What is weak today
- **Strongest ICP, but only two of five preview results had a source worth trusting.** The preview found real buyers (Bihar Education Project Council's Vidya Samiksha Kendra RFP; MAHAPREIT's empanelment EOI). Both were only search snippets, because the tender pages themselves refused or could not be read (5 refused with an access error, 3 were PDF or non-web files, 2 blocked by robots rules).
- **Heavy repetition.** Of 95 searches, many repeat the same idea with small wording changes ("school ERP tender", "school ERP implementation", "school ERP system integrator"). That wastes search budget and leaves other triggers thin, such as corrigendum, empanelment, and career guidance.
- **Only Gujarat and Maharashtra are named.** The ICP region is "India", but nearly every state-specific search says Gujarat or Maharashtra. Bihar, Odisha and Manipur came through by chance. Other states are barely searched. Also, one preview result was a social-media post (Instagram) and one a newspaper item, which are weak evidence.

### 2.3 Which sources fit
- Best fit and readable: **CPPP**, **GeM bids**, **GeM home**, **Tamil Nadu tenders**, **Kerala e-tenders**, **BidAssist**, **MyGov**, **Digital India**.
- Fit but blocked: **Maharashtra tenders** and **Gujarat nProcure** (robots rules; these two are your priority states, so a lead only), **Tender247**, **TenderDetail**.
- Fit but cannot be read: **Skill India** (needs JavaScript or login), **MSDE** (access refused).
- Little use for this ICP: Indian Railways, Gujarat Informatics, DoPT, NIELIT, NITI Aayog.

### 2.4 Rules to add (do not propose)
1. Social-media posts (Instagram, Facebook, X) as the only evidence; keep as lead, never as proposal.
2. Newspaper items about school buildings, scholarships, mid-day meals or exam results with no technology purchase.
3. Buyers outside India, and Indian private schools or school chains.
4. Tender pages older than the tender's last date, or listed as cancelled, closed or awarded already.
5. Research papers, policy think-tank reports and opinion columns about education technology.

## 3. Enterprise Brain B2G (government departments, PSUs, statutory bodies)

### 3.1 What is weak today
- **Only 14 searches against 95 for ScholarClone.** Many triggers (new department or mission created, programme leadership appointed, decision-support, HRMS modernisation) have no search at all.
- **Searches are broad and carry no state or "India".** Seven of the 14 have no India word ("government knowledge management system tender", "government data integration RFP", "government digital transformation tender"). The preview's four results were a US federal agency (OPM), another US agency (FAA), Bhutan's GovTech agency, and one Indian ministry (Tourism) backed only by a law-firm article. The ICP says Indian states first, abroad only through partners.
- **The best evidence could not be read.** Tender and RFP documents are the strongest proof. The preview could read 4 of 11 pages; the rest were refused, PDFs, or blank. A government AI vendor panel result was dropped because it was a YouTube snippet.

- Best fit and readable: **CPPP**, **Central e-tenders**, **GeM home**, **GeM bids**, **Tamil Nadu tenders**, **Kerala e-tenders**, **Digital India**, **C-DAC**, **MyGov**, **BidAssist**.

- Fit but unreadable: **DoPT** (blank without JavaScript).
- Little use: Indian Railways, Skill India, MSDE.
- Since the biggest gap is the PDF tender documents, the single most useful technical improvement would be reading PDFs. That is a build task for the team, not something you can change in the ICP.

### 3.4 Rules to add (do not propose)
1. Any buyer outside India (US, UK, Bhutan and similar), unless the page names an Indian implementation partner.
2. Opinion posts, law-firm articles, newsletters and think-tank commentary (the preview offered one).
3. Video snippets (YouTube) with no linked tender or notice.
4. Ministries or departments named only in passing, with no project, tender or programme attached.
5. Tenders for hardware, civil work, manpower supply or basic standalone websites.

## 4. What you need to do to apply this

1. Open the Product Portfolio screen and choose the ICP (G2G, ScholarClone, Enterprise Brain).
2. Add the new search rows from the tables above (query text, topic, days). You do not need to delete the old ones; I suggest removing the very short ones such as "manufacturing" and the repeated ScholarClone school ERP lines once the new ones prove better.
3. Add the "do not propose" rules to that ICP's disqualifiers list.
4. For Enterprise Brain, also consider tightening the region wording so that India states come first and overseas appears only "through partners". The preview showed the current wording lets US and Bhutan buyers through.
5. Ask for a fresh market preview and compare it with the first one. Keep the searches that return named Indian buyers and drop the rest.

Nothing here changes by itself. The new searches use more of your search budget, so adding all 40 at once may cost more per run; add a batch, preview, then decide.
