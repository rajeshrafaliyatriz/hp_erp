## Where the time actually went

1. **Waiting, not work.** The single biggest loss was the usage limit: four large agents at once exhausted the session limit, the dispatch failed, and the tracks could only restart after the reset (about 14:20). That is why the instruction was changed to "sized to the usage limit": three tracks fit, four did not. The 33-minute gap in commits (13:53 to 14:26) is mostly this plus the analysis that had to come first.
2. **Agent runs of about 20 minutes each** are real work that I could not shorten, but they ran in parallel (B1b, B2, F1 together), so wall time was about one run, not three.
3. **Full suite about 5 min**, run once per slice (not per change). The fast tier (only the S3 files: 161 tests, 70 s) ran per change.
4. **Browser walkthroughs 10+ minutes** each, dominated by the single-threaded dev server and first-hit route compilation; one server, one page at a time.
5. **My own faults cost more than any tool** (see the next section): every one of them was found by an instrument, none by luck.

## Decisions that differ from the brief (so the owner can overrule)

- **Merge: identity handling.** The brief said null the removed identity's pointers and soft-delete it. B2 instead moves its GTM and Signals pointers to the survivor where the survivor has none and soft-deletes it only when nothing is left, because nulling would orphan those links. I accepted this: it loses nothing and the property test covers it.
- **Merge: duplicate relationship edges** are hard-deleted (identical edges are the same fact twice); an edge between the two merged organisations is dropped (it would become a self-relation).
- **Rows with a NULL tenant are never moved** by the merge (cannot be proven to belong to the tenant).
- **API:** `PATCH` returns the full 360; creating a CRM organisation returns 201 when new and 200 for restore or `created:false`; a live same-name CRM organisation gives 422 and writes nothing.
