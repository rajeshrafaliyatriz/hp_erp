=== what is NOT a mutation here, and why (so a reader does not look for it)
- The administrator rule of POST /matches/decide is held by TWO independent guards (ProductMatchController::decide and ProductMerger::caller). Removing either one alone survives by design (the other still answers 403); A02 removes both and is killed. The list (GET) has one guard, A01, killed.
- The 'identity signal' requirement of MATCH-HIGH is implied by HIGH (55) being above everything the support signals can add up to (53): it can only be tested through HIGH, which S19 does (lowering HIGH to 50 is killed by ProductMatchSuggesterTest::test_the_thresholds_keep_their_relations and the verdicts).
- 'same id twice' (ProductMerger::guardInput) and the controller's 'different:itemMasterId' rule both end in a 422 from the other layer or from the kind checks; no mutation of them changes an answer.
- lockForUpdate() (the row locks) cannot be seen on sqlite: a mutation that removes it survives and is NOT claimed to be tested. It matters on MariaDB InnoDB (two administrators merging the same pair at once): the second waits, then finds the pair already merged. read in the documentation, unverified here.
- The repoint's own safety nets (the row count that must equal the rows found, and 'nothing still points at the retired record') cannot fire while the repoint is right; they are exercised only together with a broken repoint (M08, M09 are killed by the per-pointer assertions first). Removing a net alone is an equivalent mutant.
